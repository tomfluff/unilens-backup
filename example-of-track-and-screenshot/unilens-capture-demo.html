Overview

The Problem with Modern Websites

Corporate websites today are designed primarily to publish information, not to ensure that every user actually understands it. Investors, job applicants, existing customers — all encounter the same dense, one-size-fits-all pages regardless of their background, visual ability, or familiarity with the subject matter.

The result is predictable: users bounce. They leave not because the information is absent, but because the cognitive load of extracting meaning exceeds their threshold. This is not a content problem. It is a comprehension design problem.

Why It Matters

Three Layers of Failure

Web accessibility research identifies three distinct layers where information fails to reach users. Each layer demands a different intervention, and most existing tools address only the first.

① Physical Layer

Visual characteristics like contrast, font size, and color encoding are fixed and ignore the diversity of human vision. Low-vision users, elderly users, and those with color blindness receive no adaptation.

② Logical Layer

Specialized terminology, complex charts, and technical jargon create comprehension barriers that have nothing to do with visual ability. A novice investor reading an IR report faces the same wall as someone with low vision.

③ Contextual Layer

Even when users can see and read content, the information displayed may not match what they actually need at that moment. The page doesn't know what question the user is trying to answer.

The Opportunity

AI can bridge all three layers simultaneously — adapting visual presentation, simplifying language, and surfacing contextually relevant content based on inferred user intent.

Solution

The UniLens Approach

UniLens operates as a second layer on top of existing websites. It does not require any modification to the underlying site. Instead, it observes user behavior, understands page content using vision-language models, and offers targeted assistance through an unobtrusive overlay.

Crucially, all significant interventions are user-initiated. UniLens may proactively suggest assistance, but the user retains full control over whether and how the page adapts. This preserves spatial memory and avoids the disorientation that unsolicited page restructuring would cause.

Evidence

Prior Research Results

The pre-high-cycle research phase produced a working prototype for chart comprehension assistance targeted at low-vision users. The system used cursor position and viewport state to infer which part of a chart the user was examining, then surfaced relevant data and explanations in proximity.

User testing with low-vision participants rated the tool highly on three dimensions: ease of use, reliability of answers, and overall effectiveness. The work was recognized at WISS 2025 (Best Paper Award) and accepted at CHI 2026.

"The system successfully extracted primary information such as coordinate data and item details from charts, and displayed it with improved visibility." — WISS 2025 Paper
