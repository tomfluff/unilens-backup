Oven, frying and cooking temperatures

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

Celsius, Fahrenheit and gas marks, fan-oven adjustments, oil temperatures for frying, and when meat is done.

All Daily Table recipes give oven temperatures for a conventional oven (top and bottom heat, no fan). If your oven has a fan (convection) setting, lower the temperature by 20°C (about 25°F), or keep the temperature and check 5–10 minutes early. Ovens are often 10–20°C off what the dial says, so an oven thermometer (about ¥1,500) is the best ¥1,500 you will spend.

To convert yourself: °F = °C × 1.8 + 32, and °C = (°F − 32) ÷ 1.8. A Japanese toaster oven at 1,000 W runs at about 230°C, at 1,300 W about 250°C.

Oil temperatures for deep-frying

You do not need a thermometer: dip a dry wooden chopstick into the oil, or drop in a little batter, and watch.

TemperatureChopstick testBatter testUsed for
150–160°C (low)Small bubbles rise slowly from the tipSinks to the bottom, rises slowlySweet potatoes (daigaku-imo), first fry of karaage
170–180°C (medium)Steady small bubbles from the whole chopstickSinks halfway, comes straight back upAgedashi tofu, tempura, most frying
190°C and above (high)Lots of large bubbles at onceStays on the surface and sizzlesSecond fry of karaage (briefly)
Tip Fill the pan with at least 3 cm of oil and fry in small batches: adding too much cold food at once drops the oil by 20–30°C and makes it greasy.
