Rice-to-water ratios for every kind of rice

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

How much water for Japanese rice, new-crop rice, brown rice, mixed rice and porridge, in a pot, a donabe or a rice cooker.

Japanese rice is measured with a rice cup (1 gō, 合), which holds 180 ml, about 150 g of uncooked rice. Two gō make about 660 g of cooked rice: four rice bowls, or six onigiri. In a rice cooker you simply fill to the line for the number of gō; in a pot you need the ratios below.

Water for 1 gō (180 ml) of rice

RiceWater per gōRatio by volumeSoakNotes
Japanese white rice200 ml1.1×30 min (summer) to 1 hr (winter)Our everyday ratio
New-crop rice (shinmai, Sept–Nov)180 ml1.0×30 minFresh rice holds more moisture: use about 10% less water
Sushi rice180 ml1.0×30 minSlightly firm, because the vinegar is added after
Mixed rice (takikomi gohan)200 ml including the seasonings1.1×30 min in plain waterAdd soy sauce and mirin first, then top up with dashi; see the note below
Brown rice270 ml1.5×6 hrs or overnightPot: 40–45 min on low
Rice with mixed grains (zakkoku)200 ml + 2 tbsp per tbsp of grains1.1× + extra1 hrUse 1 tbsp of grain mix per gō
Long-grain (jasmine)225 ml1.25×NoneNot Japanese; for curries
Basmati270 ml1.5×20–30 minNot Japanese; for curries
Rice porridge, okayu (zen-gayu)900 ml5×30 minSimmer 40 min on the lowest heat, lid ajar
Soft porridge (shichibu-gayu)1,260 ml7×30 minFor a poorly stomach

Tip For mixed rice, the seasonings count as water. Put the drained rice in the pot, add the soy sauce, mirin and sake first, then top up with dashi to the normal amount (or to the line on the rice cooker). Do not stir in the vegetables: lay them on top, or the rice underneath cooks unevenly.

Cooking rice in a pot

  1. Rinse the rice: cover with water, swirl quickly and pour off at once (the first water is absorbed fastest). Repeat 3–4 times. The water does not need to run clear.
  2. Soak in the measured water for 30 minutes (1 hour in winter). The grains turn from translucent to opaque white.
  3. Cover and bring to the boil over medium-high heat, about 5–7 minutes.
  4. Turn the heat to its lowest and cook 12–13 minutes. Do not lift the lid.
  5. Turn the heat to high for 10 seconds to dry the bottom, then switch off.
  6. Leave covered for 10 minutes, then fluff with a wet paddle, cutting and turning from the bottom.

Cooking rice in a donabe

A donabe (clay pot) holds heat beautifully and makes a little crust (okoge) at the bottom. Use the same water, cook on medium heat until steam puffs from the lid hole (about 10–12 minutes), then on low for 15 minutes, and rest 10 minutes. Never put an empty or wet-bottomed donabe on the flame: it can crack.
