Measurement conversions: cups, spoons and grams

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

How many grams is a cup of flour? Spoon and cup sizes, weights of common ingredients, ounces and millilitres.

I write every recipe in grams because weighing is the most reliable way to cook, and because a "cup" means three different things depending on where you are. Our English editor Ryo converts every recipe to US cups using the weights below, which we measured ourselves (flour spooned into the cup and levelled with a knife, not scooped).

Spoons and cups

MeasureMillilitresNotes
1 teaspoon (tsp)5 mlJapanese 小さじ1
1 tablespoon (tbsp)15 ml= 3 tsp. Japanese 大さじ1
1 US cup240 ml= 16 tbsp. Used in our US measurements
1 Japanese measuring cup200 mlOlder Japanese recipes; we never use it
1 rice cup (1 gō, 1合)180 mlAbout 150 g of uncooked rice. Comes with every rice cooker
1 metric cup (UK, Australia)250 ml
1 US fluid ounce30 mlExactly 29.6 ml
Tip Watch out for the rice cooker cup: it holds 180 ml, not 240 ml. If a recipe says "2 cups of rice" and means rice cooker cups, that is 360 ml (300 g).

Pans and eggs

  • An 18 cm loaf pan is about 7 × 3½ inches; a 20 cm round pan is 8 inches; a 24 cm pot is 9½ inches.
  • A Japanese "1.5-kin" bread pan is about 20 × 10 × 10 cm, close to a US 9 × 4 inch Pullman pan.
  • Japanese M-size eggs weigh 58–64 g in the shell, about 50 g without: the same as a US large egg.
  • A US stick of butter is 113 g, 8 tablespoons or ½ cup. Japanese butter comes in 200 g blocks; mark it in 10 g slices.
