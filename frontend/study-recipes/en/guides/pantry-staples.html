Japanese pantry staples, and how to keep them

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

Soy sauce, mirin, sake, miso, kombu and more: what they are, how long they keep once opened, and what to use instead.

With about a dozen bottles and packets you can cook almost every recipe on this site. Here is what is in my cupboard and fridge, how I store each one, and what to use if you cannot find it. Most of these are sold at any Japanese supermarket; abroad, look in Asian grocers or on Marketa.

The essentials

StapleWhat it isAfter openingInstead, use
Soy sauce (koikuchi)Regular dark soy sauce, about 16% saltFridge; best within 1 month, safe for much longerTamari for gluten-free
Light soy sauce (usukuchi)Paler in colour but saltier, about 18% saltFridge, 1 monthRegular soy sauce with a pinch of salt
Mirin (hon-mirin)Sweet rice wine, about 14% alcoholCool cupboard, 3 months (it crystallises in the fridge)Per 1 tbsp: 1 tbsp sake + 1 tsp sugar; alcohol-free: 1 tbsp water + 1 tsp sugar
Mirin-style seasoningUnder 1% alcohol, cheaper, less flavourFridge, 3 monthsHon-mirin
SakeUse a dry drinking sake; "cooking sake" contains about 2% saltCool cupboard, 2–3 monthsDry sherry, dry white wine; water for no alcohol
Rice vinegarMild vinegar, about 4.5% acidityCupboard, 6 monthsApple cider vinegar
MisoWhite (sweet, light) or red/awase (saltier, deeper)Fridge, 6–12 months; it never freezes hard, so you can freeze it and spoon straight outAnother miso; add sugar to red miso to imitate white
KombuDried kelp for dashi; the white powder is flavour, not mouldAirtight in a dry cupboard, 1 yearKombu dashi granules
KatsuobushiShaved smoked dried bonitoSeal tightly, fridge, 1 monthInstant dashi granules
Dried shiitakeFor vegetarian dashi and simmered dishesAirtight, cupboard, 1 yearFresh shiitake (less flavour)
Potato starch (katakuriko)For crisp coatings and thickeningCupboard, 1 yearCornstarch (slightly less crisp)
PankoJapanese breadcrumbs, light and coarseCupboard, 1 month; freezer, 3 monthsDry breadcrumbs (use 20% less)
Toasted sesame oilDark, nutty, for flavour not fryingCool cupboard, 3 months–
Instant dashi granulesContain salt (about 40%)Cupboard, 6 monthsReal dashi: see How to make dashi (and the vegetarian kind)
Sake lees (sake kasu)The pressed lees from sake brewing, about 8% alcoholFridge 1 month, freezer 1 yearWhite miso + a little sake (not the same)
Yuzu koshoSalty paste of yuzu zest and chilliFridge, 6 monthsLime zest + chilli + salt
Tip Instant dashi and cooking sake both contain salt. If you use them instead of real dashi or drinking sake, cut the salt or soy sauce in the recipe by about a quarter, then taste.

Flours and starches

  • Hakurikiko (cake flour, about 8% protein): for cakes and tempura.
  • Kyōrikiko (bread flour, about 12% protein): for bread.
  • Shiratamako: glutinous rice flour, for soft, chewy dango. Mochiko is similar but finer.
  • Jōshinko: non-glutinous rice flour, for firmer dango. Mix it half and half with shiratamako for festival-style dango that hold their shape.

How I organise the fridge door

Top shelf: miso, yuzu kosho, sake lees. Middle: soy sauce, light soy sauce, mirin-style seasoning. Bottom: dashi in a 1-litre bottle (2–3 days), opened katsuobushi in a clip-top jar. If a bottle has no date, write the opening date on a piece of masking tape.
