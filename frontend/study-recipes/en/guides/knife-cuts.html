Japanese knife cuts, with diagrams

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

Twelve cuts used in Japanese recipes: what they look like, their sizes, and where we use them.

Japanese recipes name the cut rather than describe it: "cut the carrot in ran-giri" or "slice the green onion koguchi-giri". The cut matters: pieces of the same size cook in the same time, and cuts with many faces (like ran-giri) soak up a simmering sauce faster. Here are the twelve cuts used on Daily Table, with a diagram of each.

Holding the knife

Hold the handle with three fingers and pinch the blade between your thumb and the side of your index finger. The other hand makes a "cat's paw": fingertips curled under, knuckles against the flat of the blade, so the blade can never reach your fingertips. Push the knife forward and down as you cut, rather than pressing straight down.

Sizes at a glance

CutJapaneseSizeUsed in
RoundsWa-giri5 mm–2 cm thick slices across a carrot or daikon.Simmered daikon, oden
Half-moonsHangetsu-giriHalve lengthways, then slice across, 5–8 mm thick.Minato-nabe daikon
Quarter-moonsIchō-giri (ginkgo leaf)Quarter lengthways, then slice across, 3–5 mm thick.Miso soup, mushroom rice carrot
Rolling wedgesRan-giriCut on the diagonal, roll a quarter turn, cut again. Pieces about 3 cm with many cut faces, so they cook evenly and soak up sauce.Nikujaga, curry
WedgesKushigata-giriHalve an onion or tomato through the root, then cut in 6–8 wedges towards the centre.Nikujaga onion, sheet-pan onion
Fine julienneSen-giriStrips 1–2 mm wide and 4–5 cm long. For cabbage, roll several leaves and slice as thinly as you can.Cabbage for ginger pork, ginger for mackerel
Thin rectanglesTanzaku-giriPlanks 1 cm wide, 4–5 cm long and 1–2 mm thick, like a paper poem card.Miso soup, stir-fries
DiceSainome-giri1 cm cubes: cut into 1 cm sticks, then across.Soups, salads
Fine minceMijin-giriPieces of 1–2 mm. For onion, make close vertical and horizontal cuts without cutting through the root, then slice across.Hamburg steak onion, ginger, garlic
Diagonal slicesNaname-giriSlices at about 45° across a long vegetable, 5 mm–1 cm thick.Green onion for hot pot
Thin ringsKoguchi-giriRings of 1–3 mm across green onions or cucumbers.Garnish for oyakodon, agedashi tofu
ShavingsSasagakiShave thin slivers off burdock as if sharpening a pencil, rotating as you go. Drop into water at once.Burdock in minato-nabe
Tip Soak cut burdock, lotus root and eggplant in water for 5 minutes so they do not turn brown. Cut potatoes can wait in water for up to 30 minutes, but for nikujaga drain them well: the surface starch helps thicken the sauce.
