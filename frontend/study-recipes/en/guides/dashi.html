How to make dashi (and the vegetarian kind)

by Natsuki AoyamaUpdated September 12, 2026

Kombu and katsuobushi dashi in 45 minutes, cold-brew kombu dashi, kombu-shiitake dashi for vegetarians, and the instant shortcut.

Dashi is the stock behind miso soup, nikujaga, oyakodon and most of Japanese home cooking. Making it takes 45 minutes, most of it waiting, and the difference from instant granules is the first thing my children notice. My grandmother Fumi made a 5-litre pot every morning at the inn; I make one litre on Sunday night and it lasts until Wednesday.

Awase dashi, step by step

  1. Wipe the kombu lightly with a damp cloth. Do not wash it: the white powder is flavour.
  2. Soak the kombu in 1 litre of water for 30 minutes (or overnight in the fridge for a deeper flavour).
  3. Heat over low-medium heat for about 10 minutes. When tiny bubbles cling to the kombu and the edge of the pot, at about 90°C, take the kombu out. Boiling kombu makes the dashi slimy and bitter.
  4. Bring the water to the boil, add the 20 g of katsuobushi all at once and turn off the heat straight away.
  5. Leave for 2 minutes, until the flakes sink.
  6. Strain through a sieve lined with a paper towel. Do not squeeze the flakes: that makes the dashi cloudy and fishy. You get about 900 ml.

Storing dashi

  • Fridge: 2–3 days in a closed bottle (vegetarian kombu-shiitake dashi: 3 days).
  • Freezer: 3 weeks. Freeze in an ice cube tray; one standard cube is about 30 ml, 2 tablespoons.
  • Miso soup for one is 150 ml of dashi and 2 teaspoons (12 g) of miso, added off the boil.
Tip Do not throw away the used kombu: slice it thinly and simmer with soy sauce, mirin and sesame for a tsukudani to eat with rice, or keep it in the freezer until you have enough.

Recipes that start with dashi: Nikujaga (Japanese Meat and Potato Stew), Oyakodon (Chicken and Egg Rice Bowl), Agedashi Tofu (Crispy Tofu in Dashi Sauce), Tamagoyaki (Japanese Rolled Omelette), Kinoko Takikomi Gohan (Autumn Mushroom Rice).
