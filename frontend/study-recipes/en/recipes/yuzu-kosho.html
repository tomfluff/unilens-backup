Homemade Yuzu Kosho (Green Yuzu and Chilli Paste)

★★★★★ 4.9 from 147 ratings by Natsuki Aoyama Updated September 15, 2026 7 comments

A salty, fiery, wonderfully fragrant paste of green yuzu zest, green chillies and salt. Thirty minutes of grinding, one week in the fridge, and it keeps for six months.

When I was small, the last week of September at my grandmother Fumi's inn in Tsukimi Onsen meant one thing: green fingers. Fumi would send my cousins and me down to the orchards in the Kagami valley with a basket, and we came back with green yuzu the size of golf balls and a bag of fiery little green chillies from her neighbour's field. Then we sat on the engawa (the wooden veranda) grating zest while she ground it with salt in a huge Harukawa-ware suribachi, the grooved mortar that had been at the inn since before the war. The whole house smelled of citrus and chilli, and our eyes watered for an hour.

Yuzu kosho is a salty, spicy, intensely fragrant paste of yuzu zest, chillies and salt. Despite the name, there is no pepper in it: kosho means chilli in the dialects of Kyushu, where the paste comes from. It travelled here decades ago and stayed, because the Kagami valley grows some of the best yuzu in the country. Every September and October, Harukawa kitchens make a jar, and the yakitori stalls at the Lantern Festival put a dab on everything.

Why you'll love it

  • A tiny jar goes a long way: a quarter teaspoon lifts a whole bowl of soup.
  • It keeps for six months in the fridge and a year in the freezer.
  • Homemade is brighter and greener than any tube from the shop.

Green or red?

This is the classic green version, made in September and October with unripe green yuzu and green chillies; it is sharp and grassy. In November, when the yuzu turn yellow, make the red version the same way with yellow yuzu and fresh red chillies. It is rounder and a little sweeter. For both, use roughly equal weights of zest and chillies, and salt at 15–20% of their combined weight. Below 15% the paste can spoil; I use 20% for a jar I want to keep all year.

Before you start

Wear gloves. Chilli oil soaks into your skin, and it will find your eyes hours later (I learned this the hard way at nine). Wash and dry the yuzu and chillies thoroughly, because any water left on them shortens the shelf life. A kitchen scale is essential, since the salt is calculated from the weight of the zest and chillies. I buy green yuzu at the vegetable stalls of the Minori Port morning market, about 70 yen each in early October, and the chillies from the same stall. Seven small green yuzu give about 40 g of zest.

How to use it

Stir a little into miso soup, dab it on grilled chicken or steak, mix it into mayonnaise for a dip, or put it on the table with minato-nabe or any hot pot. It is also wonderful in my butter-soy mushroom pasta and on karaage. Keep the juice of the zested yuzu for yuzu miso salmon.

Homemade Yuzu Kosho (Green Yuzu and Chilli Paste)

Ingredients

Quantities in the instructions are for the original recipe.

Equipment

  • Disposable gloves (nitrile)
  • Fine grater or zester
  • Kitchen scale (accurate to 1 g)
  • Suribachi and surikogi (Japanese grooved mortar and wooden pestle), or a small food processor
  • Glass jar of about 120 ml with a lid, sterilised

Instructions

  1. Put on disposable gloves and keep them on until the jar is closed. Wash the yuzu and chillies, dry them with a clean towel, then leave them on a tray for 10 minutes so every last drop of water evaporates.

  2. Grate the green outer skin of the yuzu on a fine grater, turning the fruit as you go so you take only the thin coloured layer; stop as soon as you see white pith, which is bitter. Weigh the zest (aim for about 40 g). Squeeze the zested fruit and keep the juice.

  3. Cut the stems off the chillies, halve them lengthwise and scrape out the seeds and white ribs with a teaspoon for a milder paste (leave some in if you like it hot). Chop finely and weigh: aim for the same weight as the zest.

  4. Add the two weights together and work out the salt at 15–20% of the total. For 40 g zest and 40 g chillies (80 g), that is 12–16 g salt. Use the higher amount if you want to keep the jar for more than three months.

  5. Suribachi: grind the chillies with the salt for about 5 minutes until they break down into a rough paste, then add the zest and grind for another 5–10 minutes, scraping the grooves with a spoon, until smooth and evenly green. Food processor: process everything in 20-second bursts for 2–3 minutes, scraping down the sides; a small batch can stay coarse, so finish it in a suribachi if you want it smooth.

  6. Taste a tiny bit on the tip of a spoon. If the paste is very stiff, loosen it with 1–2 tsp yuzu juice. More juice makes it softer but shortens its life a little.

  7. Pack the paste into a sterilised jar (boil the jar and lid for 5 minutes, or wipe them with 70% alcohol, and let them dry). Press it down with a spoon to push out any air pockets, smooth the top and press plastic wrap onto the surface before closing the lid.

  8. Leave it in the fridge for at least 1 week before using. The salt pulls the flavours together, the raw heat of the chilli mellows, and the colour deepens from bright to darker green. Always take it out with a clean, dry spoon.

Notes

  • The salt is what keeps the paste safe. Never go below 15% of the combined weight of zest and chillies.
  • Chillies vary hugely in heat. Taste a sliver of one before you start; if they are very hot, remove all the seeds and ribs, or use fewer chillies (down to 30 g) and recalculate the salt on the new total.
  • If your hands burn after handling chillies, rub them with a little vegetable oil and then wash with dish soap. Capsaicin dissolves in fat, not water.
  • A slightly darker layer on top is just oxidation and is fine. White or fuzzy spots are mould: throw the whole jar away.
  • Over the months the colour fades from bright to olive green; the flavour stays.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
Green yuzuSudachi, kabosu or lime zest (lime makes a sharper 'lime kosho')
Green chillies (ao-tōgarashi)Green serrano or jalapeño (milder and juicier); with Thai bird's eye chillies, use about 30 g, as they are much hotter
Fine saltFine sea salt; grind coarse salt first, or it won't dissolve into the paste
SuribachiA small food processor, or a stone mortar and pestle

Make ahead

You can zest the yuzu up to a month ahead: freeze the zest flat in a zip-top bag and thaw it in the fridge before grinding. Chop and weigh everything before you start grinding, so you only take your gloves off once.

Storage

Keeps for 6 months in the fridge in a clean jar with plastic wrap pressed onto the surface; always use a clean, dry spoon. For longer, freeze teaspoon-sized portions wrapped in plastic wrap (or in an ice cube tray) for up to a year. The salt stops it freezing hard, so you can scoop it straight from the freezer.
