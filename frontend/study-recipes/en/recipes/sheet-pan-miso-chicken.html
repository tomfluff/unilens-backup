Sheet-Pan Miso Chicken with Kabocha

Chicken thighs, sweet kabocha wedges, red onion and shiitake roasted on one tray, with a sticky miso-honey glaze brushed on for the last 10 minutes. A 15-minute-prep weeknight dinner that makes the best next-day bento.

There is a particular kind of Tuesday in October in our house. Hana has volleyball until six, Sota has lost one sock and both of his library books, Daisuke messages from the Bayside Line that the train is stuck at Minori Port again, and I am standing in the kitchen at 5:40 with a pack of chicken thighs, a quarter of a kabocha and no plan at all. This sheet-pan miso chicken was born on one of those evenings, and it has rescued me about once a week every autumn since.

Everything goes onto one tray: chicken thighs, wedges of kabocha, red onion and fat shiitake. Twenty-five minutes in a hot oven, then a sticky miso glaze, then ten minutes more. My grandmother Fumi used to brush sweet miso on grilled eggplant and tofu (dengaku) for the guests at her inn in Tsukimi Onsen, and the smell of miso and mirin catching on a hot surface is, to me, the smell of autumn evenings there. This is the lazy weeknight version of that smell.

Why you'll love it

  • One tray, one small bowl and about 15 minutes of hands-on work.
  • The kabocha roasts in the chicken fat and comes out caramelised at the edges and creamy inside.
  • Leftovers make a wonderful lunchbox the next day, cold or reheated.

Choosing the kabocha

In October the Kagami Valley farmers' market by the river is piled high with kabocha from the farms below Mount Tsukimi. Pick one that feels heavy for its size, with a dry, corky stem and deep orange flesh if it's already cut; plump seeds mean it was fully ripe. A quarter of a medium kabocha (about 400 g) is plenty for four. Leave the skin on: it's edible, and it holds the wedges together in the oven.

Kabocha is hard to cut, so here is my trick: microwave the piece at 600 W for 2–3 minutes and the knife goes through far more easily (mind the steam). Cut wedges about 1.5 cm thick at the widest part so they cook in the same time as the chicken. My knife cuts guide shows how to cut wedges safely, and the seasonal produce guide lists what else is good right now.

Tips for success

Pat the chicken dry and sit it skin side up in the gaps between the vegetables, not on top of them, so the skin crisps instead of steaming. My oven-microwave is small and has no fan; if your tray is crowded, use two trays and swap them halfway, because crowded vegetables steam rather than roast. I bake at 220°C (430°F), or 200°C (390°F) in a fan oven.

The one rule that matters: the glaze goes on only for the last 10 minutes. Miso, mirin and honey are full of sugar and burn quickly at this heat; brush them on at the start and you get black, bitter patches. If the edges darken too fast at the end, move the tray down a shelf. The chicken is done at 75°C (167°F) in the thickest part, or when the juices run clear. I use awase (mixed) miso; red miso is saltier, so see the notes before you start.

Sheet-Pan Miso Chicken with Kabocha

Equipment

  • Large oven tray (about 30 × 40 cm), or two smaller trays
  • Baking paper
  • Pastry brush or a spoon
  • Meat thermometer (optional, but useful)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 220°C (430°F), or 200°C (390°F) for a fan oven, and line a large oven tray with baking paper. Pat the chicken dry with kitchen paper, prick the skin a few times with a fork, cut each thigh in half and season all over with the ½ tsp salt.

  2. If the kabocha is too hard to cut, microwave it at 600 W for 2–3 minutes first. Cut it into wedges about 1.5 cm thick, cut the red onion into 8 wedges through the root so they hold together, and halve the shiitake.

  3. Toss the kabocha and onion with the oil and a pinch of salt right on the tray, then spread them out in one layer. Nestle the chicken skin side up in the gaps, leaving a little space around each piece; if anything overlaps, use a second tray.

  4. Roast on the middle shelf for 25 minutes, until the chicken skin is golden and the kabocha is starting to soften. If you are using two trays, swap them over after 12 minutes.

  5. Meanwhile, stir all the glaze ingredients together in a small bowl until smooth and glossy, and toss the shiitake in 1 tbsp of it.

  6. Take out the tray and brush the rest of the glaze over the chicken skin and the cut faces of the kabocha. Add the shiitake and roast for another 10 minutes, until the glaze bubbles and browns in spots. If it darkens too fast, move the tray down a shelf.

  7. Check the chicken: it should read 75°C (167°F) in the thickest part, or the juices should run clear when pierced. If not, give it 3–5 minutes more, covered loosely with foil if the glaze is already dark. Let it rest on the tray for 5 minutes.

  8. Scatter over the green onion and sesame seeds. Serve with rice, spooning the juices from the tray over the top, and put the shichimi on the table.

Notes

  • The glaze contains miso, mirin and honey, which burn quickly at 220°C. That is why it goes on only for the last 10 minutes. A few dark edges are delicious; black and bitter means move the tray lower next time.
  • Salt levels: red miso (aka miso) is saltier than awase miso, so use 2½ tbsp. With sweet white miso (shiro miso), use 4 tbsp and leave out the honey.
  • Bone-in thighs need about 10 minutes longer before glazing (35 minutes); check for 75°C right next to the bone.
  • The kabocha is done when a skewer slides into the thickest wedge with no resistance.
  • Use baking paper rather than foil: the glaze sticks to foil and tears the kabocha skin when you lift it.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
KabochaButternut squash or sweet potato, in 1.5 cm wedges; the timing stays the same
Mirin1 tbsp sake + 1 tsp sugar for each tbsp of mirin (alcohol-free: 1 tbsp water + 1 tsp sugar)
Honey1 tbsp sugar or maple syrup
Chicken thighsBone-in thighs (35 minutes before glazing) or chicken breast in 4 cm chunks, added to the tray after 10 minutes so it doesn't dry out
ShiitakeEryngii (king oyster) or maitake mushrooms

Make ahead

Mix the glaze up to 3 days ahead and keep it in a jar in the fridge. The night before, you can cut the kabocha and onion and salt the chicken; keep them covered in the fridge, and the next evening everything goes straight onto the tray.

Storage

Keeps 3 days in the fridge in a covered container. Reheat one portion in the microwave at 600 W for 1½–2 minutes until piping hot, or in a toaster oven to crisp the skin again. The chicken freezes well for a month; the kabocha turns a little watery after freezing but is fine mashed into a lunchbox.
