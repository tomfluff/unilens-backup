Yuzu Miso Salmon

★★★★★ 4.7 from 298 ratings by Natsuki Aoyama Updated September 18, 2026 8 comments

Salmon marinated in sweet white miso with Mirror valley yuzu juice and zest, then grilled until the edges caramelise. Ten minutes of work, and it waits happily in the fridge overnight.

If you take the Mirror Line inland from Springvale Central, about forty minutes in the valley opens up and the hillsides turn into terraces of dark, glossy yuzu trees. That's where I grew up, and in early October the whole valley smells faintly of citrus. The fruit is still mostly green now; it turns golden in November, just in time for the winter yuzu baths at Moonview Spa. My grandmother Fumi used to say the green ones are for the nose and the yellow ones for the tongue, and she was right: early-season yuzu has a sharper, more floral zest and a little less juice.

This salmon is what I make on evenings when swimming lessons, homework and a late train all collide. It takes ten minutes of hands-on work, you can marinate it in the morning (or the night before), and Hana packs the leftovers in her bento the next day. The marinade is a simple Springvale-style yuzu miso: sweet white miso, a little mirin and sugar, and plenty of yuzu juice and zest. I buy my yuzu from the Tamura family's farm stand outside Moonview Spa station whenever I visit my mother, and freeze the juice in ice cube trays so I have it all year.

Why you'll love it

  • Ten minutes of work, and it cooks while the rice steams.
  • Sweet, salty and fragrant, with caramelised edges.
  • Three ways to cook it: fish grill, oven or frying pan.

Choosing the salmon

Use raw, unsalted salmon (nama-zake), not the salted shio-zake sold right next to it in Japanese supermarkets, or the result will be far too salty. In autumn our fishmongers have akizake, wild chum salmon caught as it returns to the rivers: it is leaner, so take care not to overcook it. Farmed Atlantic salmon is richer and more forgiving. Fillets about 2 cm thick cook most evenly.

The one rule: wipe it off

Miso and sugar burn quickly, so scrape the marinade off before cooking, leaving only a thin film. It feels like throwing the flavour away, but the fish has already soaked it up, and a thick coat turns black long before the salmon is cooked. If your fish grill browns fast, lay a piece of foil loosely over the top for the last few minutes.

No yuzu?

Mix equal parts lemon and lime juice, with lemon zest. Sudachi or kabosu are lovely too. Be careful with bottled yuzu juice: some brands (often labelled yuzu-su) contain salt, so taste it first and use a little less miso if it is salty. If you have green yuzu left over, make a jar of yuzu kosho or bake my yuzu pound cake. Serve the salmon with rice, miso soup and something green; in autumn I love it with mushroom rice.

Yuzu Miso Salmon

Equipment

  • Fine grater or zester (a Microplane or a Japanese oroshigane)
  • Plastic wrap or a zip-top bag
  • Fish grill, or an oven and a baking tray, or a 26 cm frying pan with a lid
  • Baking paper (for the oven and frying-pan methods)

Instructions

  1. Pat the salmon dry with kitchen paper and run your fingertips over it to check for pin bones (pull any out with tweezers). Sprinkle the sake over both sides, leave for 5 minutes, then pat dry again; this takes away any fishy smell.

  2. In a small bowl, mix the miso, mirin, sugar, yuzu juice and zest until smooth.

  3. Spread half the marinade on a sheet of plastic wrap, lay the salmon on it and spread the rest over the top. Wrap snugly (or use a zip-top bag and press out the air) and refrigerate for at least 15 minutes, or up to overnight. After 24 hours the fish becomes too salty and firm.

  4. Scrape the marinade off with a spatula, then wipe gently with kitchen paper so only a thin film remains, especially on the skin. Throw the used marinade away; it has had raw fish in it.

  5. Fish grill: preheat for 1 minute. In a double-sided grill, cook skin side up over medium-low heat for 7–8 minutes without turning. In a single-sided grill, cook skin side up for 5 minutes, turn and cook 3 minutes more. If the top browns too fast, lay foil loosely over it. Add the shishito peppers for the last 3 minutes.

  6. Oven: heat to 200°C (400°F), or 180°C for a fan oven. Lay the salmon skin side up on a baking tray lined with baking paper, with the shishito peppers around it, and bake for 12–14 minutes.

  7. Frying pan: line the pan with baking paper and put the salmon in skin side down. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 4 minutes, then turn, add the shishito peppers, cover again and cook for 3–4 minutes more. The paper stops the miso from sticking and burning, so you need no oil.

  8. The salmon is done when the flesh has turned from translucent to opaque pale pink and flakes at the thickest part. Top with thin strips of yuzu peel and serve right away.

Notes

  • Marinating time changes the result: 15 minutes gives a light, fresh yuzu flavour; overnight gives a deeper, sweeter and firmer fish, close to saikyō-yaki.
  • Grate only the yellow-green outer skin of the yuzu. The white pith underneath is bitter.
  • Caramelised brown edges are normal and delicious. Black, bitter patches mean too much marinade was left on or the heat was too high.
  • If you only have regular white miso (not the sweet saikyō kind), add an extra ½ tsp sugar. With red miso, use 1½ tbsp miso and 2 tsp sugar.
  • For a bento, let the salmon cool completely before packing. It tastes good cold.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
Yuzu juiceEqual parts lemon and lime juice, or sudachi or kabosu juice
Yuzu zestLemon zest, or half lemon and half lime zest
Mirin1 tbsp sake + 1 tsp sugar (alcohol-free: 1 tbsp water + 1 tsp sugar)
SalmonSpanish mackerel (sawara), cod or yellowtail; or chicken thighs, baked at 200°C (400°F) for 18–20 minutes to 75°C in the centre
Sweet white misoRegular white miso + an extra ½ tsp sugar

Make ahead

Marinate the salmon the night before (or in the morning) and keep it wrapped in the fridge, ready to cook when you get home. The marinade itself keeps for a week in a clean jar in the fridge.

Storage

Cooked salmon keeps for 2 days in the fridge; eat it cold in a bento or warm it covered in the microwave at 600 W for about 40 seconds. Raw salmon in its marinade freezes for up to 3 weeks; thaw it overnight in the fridge.
