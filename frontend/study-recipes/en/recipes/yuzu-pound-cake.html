Yuzu Pound Cake

★★★★★ 4.6 from 198 ratings by Natsuki Aoyama Updated September 29, 2026 7 comments
Jump to Recipe

A classic equal-weights butter cake baked in an 18 cm loaf pan, full of Springvale yuzu: zest in the batter, a yuzu syrup brushed on while it's hot, and a tangy yuzu glaze on top. Even better the next day.

When I was a girl, the first yellow yuzu of the year arrived at my grandmother Fumi's inn in Moonview Spa in a crate from a farmer down in the Mirror valley. She floated a few in the big bath for the guests, grated the zest into almost everything, and on Sunday afternoons she baked a butter cake that made the whole inn smell like a yuzu orchard. This is my version of that cake.

Every year I bake one for the Lantern Festival. We take thick slices down to the Mirror River and eat them on the steps while we wait for the lanterns to be lit. This year that's Saturday 10 October, so the yuzu in my kitchen right now are from the Ishida family's orchard below Mount Moonview: early-season fruit, still half green, sharp and very fragrant.

Why you'll love it

  • A buttery, close-textured cake with yuzu in three layers: zest in the batter, syrup soaked in, glaze on top.
  • The classic equal-weights formula, so it's easy to remember and scale.
  • It keeps for days and freezes well, so it's the perfect make-ahead gift.

Choosing and using yuzu

A yuzu has much more zest and much less juice than a lemon: one gives only about 15–20 ml, so you need three for this cake. Grate only the yellow (or green) skin with a fine grater; the white pith underneath is bitter. Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingers before you add it to the butter, and the oils will scent the whole cake. If you can't find yuzu, see the substitutions; and my seasonal produce guide shows when green and yellow yuzu are in season.

Weights and cups

A pound cake is traditionally equal weights of butter, sugar, egg and flour: here 100 g of each, which is exactly two Japanese M eggs. I really recommend weighing. If you use US cups, 100 g sugar is ½ cup and 100 g cake flour is about ¾ cup plus 1 tablespoon (125 g per cup, spooned and levelled); the conversions guide has more.

Tips for success

Everything at room temperature. Take the butter and eggs out an hour ahead. The butter should be about 20°C: your finger pushes in easily, but it isn't shiny or greasy. Cold eggs are the main cause of curdled batter, the lumpy, watery look that happens when the butter can't hold the egg. Add the eggs a little at a time; if it does split, beat in a tablespoon of the measured flour, or hold the bowl over warm water for 10 seconds while you beat. It comes back.

Bake at 170°C (340°F), or 150°C (300°F) in a fan oven. After 10 minutes I slash the top down the middle, so the cake cracks neatly instead of wherever it likes. It's done when a skewer in the centre comes out clean, usually at 45–50 minutes in my oven-microwave. Don't open the door before 30 minutes (apart from the quick slash), or it may sink. My oven temperatures guide explains why ovens without a fan run a little slower.

For more yuzu, try my yuzu miso salmon, or make yuzu kosho with the leftover green fruit.

Yuzu Pound Cake

★★★★★ 4.6 from 198 ratings

A classic equal-weights butter cake baked in an 18 cm loaf pan, full of Springvale yuzu: zest in the batter, a yuzu syrup brushed on while it's hot, and a tangy yuzu glaze on top. Even better the next day.

Equipment

  • 18 cm loaf (pound cake) pan, about 18 × 8 × 6 cm
  • Baking paper
  • Digital kitchen scale
  • Hand mixer (or a whisk and patience)
  • Fine grater (Microplane) for the zest
  • Bamboo skewer
  • Pastry brush
  • Cooling rack

Instructions

  1. An hour ahead, take out the butter and eggs. Line the loaf pan with baking paper, grate the zest of 2 yuzu (yellow or green part only) and squeeze the juice of all 3. Sift the cake flour, baking powder and salt together. Preheat the oven to 170°C (340°F), or 150°C (300°F) for a fan oven.

  2. Rub the yuzu zest into the 100 g sugar with your fingertips until fragrant. Beat the butter with a hand mixer for 1 minute until creamy, then add the sugar in 3 additions and beat on high for 3–4 minutes, until pale, almost white, and fluffy.

  3. Add the beaten eggs about 1 tbsp at a time (6–8 additions), beating for 30 seconds after each until fully absorbed. If the batter starts to look lumpy and watery (curdled), beat in 1 tbsp of the sifted flour, or warm the bottom of the bowl over warm water for 10 seconds while beating, then carry on.

  4. Beat in the 1 tbsp yuzu juice for the batter; it may look slightly split, which the flour will fix. Sift in the flour mixture and fold with a spatula, cutting down through the middle and lifting from the bottom while turning the bowl, about 40–50 strokes, until no dry flour shows and the batter is glossy.

  5. Scrape the batter into the pan, level it, then push it up towards the ends so the middle is slightly lower. Tap the pan twice on the counter to knock out large bubbles. Bake on the middle shelf for 10 minutes, until a thin skin has formed on top.

  6. Quickly open the oven and slash the top lengthwise down the centre, about 1 cm deep, with a knife dipped in oil. Close the door and bake for another 35–40 minutes (45–50 in total). If the top browns too fast after 30 minutes, lay a sheet of foil over it. Meanwhile, make the syrup: microwave the 2 tbsp yuzu juice, 25 g sugar and 1 tbsp water at 600 W for 20–30 seconds and stir until the sugar dissolves.

  7. Test with a skewer in the centre: it should come out clean, and the crack should look dry and golden, not wet. If there's wet batter on the skewer, bake 5 minutes more and test again.

  8. Lift the cake out of the pan onto a rack, peel the paper away from the sides, and brush the hot syrup all over the top and sides; it soaks right in. When it is barely warm (about 1 hour), wrap it tightly in cling film so it stays moist.

  9. To glaze, stir the powdered sugar with the 2 tsp yuzu juice into a thick paste that falls from the spoon in a ribbon; add juice a few drops at a time if it's too stiff. Drizzle it over the cooled cake and let it set for 30 minutes before slicing. For the best flavour, wait until the next day.

Notes

  • Japanese M eggs are about 50 g without the shell. If your two beaten eggs weigh more than 100 g, leave a spoonful out rather than adding more of everything.
  • Curdled batter isn't ruined: beat in 1 tbsp of the measured flour, or warm the bottom of the bowl over 40°C water for 10 seconds while beating. If the butter has gone oily instead, chill the bowl for 5 minutes.
  • Fold in the flour with about 40–50 strokes, just until no dry flour shows and the batter turns glossy. Too few leaves streaks and tunnels; too many makes the cake tough.
  • A skewer in the centre should come out clean, with no wet batter (a few moist crumbs are fine). If it's wet, bake 5 minutes more and test again. If the top browns too fast after 30 minutes, lay a sheet of foil over it.
  • Sinking in the middle usually means underbaked, or the door was opened too early.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
YuzuMeyer lemon, or half lemon and half lime; bottled 100% yuzu juice works for the juice, with lemon zest for the aroma
Cake flourAll-purpose flour (slightly denser), or 85 g all-purpose flour + 15 g cornflour (cornstarch)
Unsalted butterSalted butter; leave out the pinch of salt
Powdered sugarSkip the glaze and brush on a little extra syrup instead

Make ahead

Pound cake is better on day two, when the syrup has spread through and the yuzu flavour deepens. Bake it 1–2 days ahead, brush with the syrup, wrap it tightly in cling film once it is barely warm, and glaze it on the day you serve it.

Storage

Wrapped in cling film, it keeps 4–5 days at cool room temperature; in hot weather keep it in the fridge and let slices come back to room temperature before eating. Freeze unglazed slices individually wrapped for up to a month and thaw for 1 hour at room temperature. The glaze turns sticky and weeps after freezing, so glaze after thawing.
