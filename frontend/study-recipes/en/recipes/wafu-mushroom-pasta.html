Butter-Soy Mushroom Pasta (Wafu Pasta)

★★★★★ 4.6 from 198 ratings by Natsuki Aoyama Updated September 24, 2026 7 comments

Spaghetti tossed with deeply browned autumn mushrooms, garlic, butter and a splash of soy sauce, finished with crisp nori. A 20-minute Japanese-style pasta.

Wednesdays are our most chaotic evening. Sota has swimming until six, Hana has piano until half past, and Daisuke is never home before seven. For years I tried to have something slow-cooked ready, and for years I failed. Then one October afternoon I came home from the Saturday market with far too many mushrooms, and this pasta happened. It has been our Wednesday dinner every autumn since. Kenta, who tests every recipe on this site, says he now makes it twice a week, which I take as the highest praise.

Wafū pasta means Japanese-style pasta. It grew up in Japan's coffee shops in the decades after the war, where cooks started tossing spaghetti with soy sauce, butter, nori, cod roe and whatever else was in the kitchen. Butter and soy sauce, batā-jōyu, is one of the great Japanese flavour pairings: we put it on corn, on scallops, on rice. With browned mushrooms it tastes deep and savoury, almost meaty.

Why you'll love it

  • It takes 20 minutes and one frying pan plus a pot.
  • Any mix of mushrooms works, so it is a good use for odds and ends.
  • It is vegetarian, and easy to make vegan.

Choosing the mushrooms

I buy mine from the Mirror Kinoko-en stall at the Saturday market by Springvale Central. They grow shimeji, maitake and shiitake on logs in the valley below Mount Moonview. A mix gives the most interesting texture: shimeji stay bouncy, maitake go crisp and frilly at the edges, shiitake are meaty, and king oyster mushrooms (eringi) are almost like scallops. Don't wash them; wipe off any dirt with damp kitchen paper. Wet mushrooms steam instead of browning.

Leave them alone

This is the whole secret. Spread the mushrooms in one layer in a hot pan and don't touch them for three minutes. They will squeak and hiss and you will want to stir; resist. When you finally turn them, the undersides should be deep golden brown. That colour is the flavour. If your pan is crowded they will steam grey, so for four servings brown them in two batches. And don't salt them yet: salt draws out their water.

Tips for success

Salt the pasta water properly, at 1% of the water: 20 g of salt in 2 litres. The spaghetti seasons itself as it cooks, so the sauce needs only a little soy sauce. Cook the pasta a minute less than the packet says, because it finishes in the pan. Pour the soy sauce onto the hot side of the pan, not over the mushrooms; it sizzles and caramelises for a toasty smell. Before draining, scoop out a mugful of pasta water. Its starch turns the butter and soy into a glossy sauce that clings.

Butter-Soy Mushroom Pasta (Wafu Pasta)

Equipment

  • Large pot (at least 3 L) for the pasta
  • Large frying pan (about 26 cm)
  • Tongs
  • Kitchen scissors for the nori
  • Mug for the pasta water

Instructions

  1. Bring the 2 litres of water to a rolling boil in a large pot over high heat and add the 20 g salt. Meanwhile, cut the root ends off the mushrooms; break the shimeji into small clumps, tear the maitake into bite-sized pieces, slice the shiitake 5 mm thick and cut the king oyster mushrooms into 5 cm strips.

  2. Add the spaghetti and stir for the first minute so it doesn't stick. Cook it 1 minute less than the packet time (6 minutes for a 7-minute 1.6 mm spaghetti).

  3. While the pasta cooks, put the olive oil and garlic into the cold frying pan and heat over medium-low for 1–2 minutes, until fragrant and pale gold at the edges. Turn the heat to medium-high, add the mushrooms in one layer and leave them completely alone for 3 minutes, until the undersides are deep golden. Then toss and cook for 1–2 minutes more.

  4. Turn the heat down to medium. Add the butter, and as it melts, pour 1 tbsp of the soy sauce onto an empty, hot part of the pan so it sizzles for a few seconds before you toss it through the mushrooms. Before draining the pasta, scoop out a mugful (about 150 ml) of the cooking water.

  5. Drain the spaghetti, or lift it straight into the frying pan with tongs, and add about 80 ml of the pasta water. Toss over medium heat for about 1 minute, until the butter, soy sauce and water turn into a glossy sauce that clings to the pasta; add another splash of water if it looks dry. Taste, add the last ½ tbsp soy sauce if needed, and season with black pepper.

  6. Divide between warm plates, top with the nori strips and shredded shiso and eat straight away.

Notes

  • The pasta water should taste pleasantly salty, like a mild soup. If you use less salt, add a little more soy sauce at the end.
  • If the sauce looks oily or separated, add another splash of pasta water and toss hard; it will come together.
  • Cut the nori with scissors right before serving and scatter it at the table so it stays crisp.
  • Yuzu kosho variation: stir ½ tsp yuzu kosho into the butter in step 4 and use only 1 tbsp soy sauce, because yuzu kosho is salty.
  • For greens, add a handful of spinach or komatsuna to the pan with the pasta; it wilts in the 1-minute toss.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
Butter (vegan)1 more tbsp olive oil plus ½ tsp white miso stirred into the soy sauce
Mixed mushroomsAny single variety, or button and chestnut mushrooms; 200 g in total
Spaghetti (gluten-free)Gluten-free spaghetti, with tamari instead of soy sauce
NoriShredded shiso, thinly sliced green onion or toasted sesame seeds

Make ahead

This is best made fresh. You can trim and tear the mushrooms in the morning; keep them in a paper bag in the fridge, not a sealed plastic box, where they go slimy.

Storage

Leftovers keep 1 day in the fridge. Reheat in a frying pan with 2 tbsp water, tossing until hot, and add fresh nori. Cooked pasta does not freeze well, but raw mushrooms do: see the comments.
