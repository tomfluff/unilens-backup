Agedashi Tofu (Crispy Tofu in Dashi Sauce)

★★★★★ 4.6 from 189 ratings by Natsuki Aoyama Updated September 8, 2026 7 comments
Jump to Recipe

Silky tofu in a thin, crisp potato-starch coat, served in a warm kombu dashi sauce with grated daikon and ginger. Vegan, and ready in 25 minutes.

Just around the corner from Springvale Central station, on the narrow street behind the old covered arcade, there's a tofu shop that opens at 6 am. Ishikawa Tofu has made tofu there since 1951, with soybeans from Harvest farms and nigari from Harvest Bay salt, and on cold mornings you can see the steam rising from the soy-milk vats through the window. Mrs. Ishikawa slides each block into a bag of water for me and always says the same thing: “Eat it today.”

So on tofu days we have agedashi tofu: tender cubes of tofu with a thin, crisp coat of potato starch, sitting in a warm pool of dashi sauce with grated daikon and ginger on top. My grandmother Fumi served it at her inn in Moonview Spa as a little extra for guests coming back from the evening bath, and Daisuke orders it at every izakaya we visit. Made with kombu dashi, as I do here, it's completely vegetarian, even vegan.

Why you'll love it

  • Crisp outside, silky inside, and about 25 minutes from start to finish.
  • Vegan as written, with a kombu dashi sauce.
  • It uses one block of tofu and things you probably already have.

Which tofu?

I use medium-firm (momen-style) tofu, which holds its shape in the oil but stays tender inside. Silken tofu gives an even softer, custardy result, but it's fragile: drain it for 20 minutes and move it with a spatula, not chopsticks. Avoid extra-firm tofu here; it turns chewy.

Drain it, really

Tofu is mostly water, and water plus hot oil means spatters and a soggy coating. Wrap the block in two layers of paper towel and set a plate on top for at least 10 minutes; 20 is better. In a hurry, microwave the wrapped tofu at 600 W for 2 minutes and let it cool a little.

Tips for success

Coat the tofu just before it goes into the oil. Potato starch soaks up moisture within minutes and turns into a gummy paste, so I dust each piece as I go. Fry at 180°C (350°F) for 2–3 minutes, turning once, and leave the pieces alone for the first minute so the coating sets and doesn't stick. Pour the sauce around the tofu, not over it, so the top stays crisp. And serve straight away: agedashi tofu waits for nobody.

Equipment

  • Small frying pan (about 20 cm) or small pot, for frying
  • Deep-fry thermometer
  • Paper towels and a flat plate, for draining the tofu
  • Small saucepan for the sauce
  • Fine grater for the daikon and ginger
  • Slotted spoon or wire skimmer, and shallow serving bowls

Instructions

  1. Wrap the tofu in two layers of paper towel, set it on a plate or tray, and put a flat plate (about 300 g) on top. Leave it to drain for at least 10 minutes, 20 if you have time. In a hurry, microwave the wrapped tofu at 600 W for 2 minutes instead and let it cool a little.

  2. Make the sauce: put the dashi, soy sauce and mirin in a small saucepan, bring to the boil over medium heat and simmer for 1 minute to cook off the alcohol. Keep it warm on the lowest heat. Grate the daikon and squeeze it lightly so it's moist but not dripping, grate the ginger, and slice the green onion.

  3. Pour oil into the frying pan to a depth of 2–3 cm and heat it over medium heat to 180°C (350°F); dry chopsticks dipped in should send up lots of fine, brisk bubbles. Meanwhile, cut the tofu into 4 equal cubes (or 6 smaller ones) and pat each one dry with fresh paper towel.

  4. Put the potato starch on a plate. Just before frying, roll a piece of tofu in it to coat every side and pat off the excess. Coat only the pieces that are going into the oil right now.

  5. Slide the pieces gently into the oil, away from you, no more than 4 at a time in a 20 cm pan. Leave them alone for the first minute, then turn them once and fry for 2–3 minutes in total, until the coating is crisp and pale golden and the tofu springs back when pressed. Lift out and drain on paper towel or a rack for 30 seconds.

  6. Place the tofu in shallow bowls and pour the hot sauce around it, not over it, so the tops stay crisp. Top with the grated daikon, ginger and green onion, add a pinch of shichimi if you like, and serve immediately.

Notes

  • Quick kombu dashi: soak 5 g kombu in 200 ml water for 30 minutes, or heat it over low heat for about 10 minutes and lift out the kombu just before the water boils (boiled kombu turns slimy and bitter). You'll get about 180 ml, enough for the sauce.
  • If the tofu sticks to the pan or the coating comes off, the oil wasn't hot enough or you moved the pieces too soon.
  • If the oil spits, the tofu is still wet. Pat it dry again and lower each piece in gently with a spoon, away from you.
  • If you're shallow-frying, the oil must come at least halfway up the tofu; turn the pieces so every side gets crisp.
  • Not cooking for vegetarians? Use bonito dashi and top with a pinch of katsuobushi flakes, the way most izakaya serve it.

Substitutions

Instead ofUse
Potato starchCornstarch (similar crunch, a little heavier) or rice flour
Kombu dashi½ tsp kombu dashi granules in 150 ml hot water, or bonito dashi if it doesn't need to be vegetarian
Mirin1½ tbsp sake + 1½ tsp sugar (alcohol-free: 1½ tbsp water + 1½ tsp sugar)
Medium-firm tofuSilken tofu: drain it for 20 minutes and handle it with a spatula
DaikonLeave it out and add a little more ginger and green onion

Make ahead

The sauce keeps for 3 days in the fridge; reheat it just before serving. You can drain the tofu in the morning, wrap it in fresh paper towel and refrigerate it. Fry it only at the last minute: the coating doesn't keep.

Storage

Best eaten at once. Leftovers keep for 1 day in the fridge, but the coating goes soft; reheat in a toaster oven for 3–4 minutes, or simmer the pieces gently in the sauce for 2 minutes (soft, but delicious). Don't freeze it: frozen tofu turns spongy.
