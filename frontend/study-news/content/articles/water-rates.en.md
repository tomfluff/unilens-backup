Harukawa households will pay an average of 8.5% more for water and sewerage from 1 April 2027 if the city council approves a tariff revision at its plenary session on 30 September, after the council's construction and waterworks committee backed the plan on 18 September by seven votes to three.

It would be the first rise since 2009. For a two-person household using 20 cubic metres every two months, the bill would go up from ¥5,830 to ¥6,325, an increase of ¥495. A single person using 10 cubic metres would pay ¥220 more, and a family of four using 40 cubic metres ¥930 more. Because the new tariff raises the charge per cubic metre more steeply for heavier use, a small shop using 100 cubic metres would face a 9.8% rise, or ¥2,750 every two months.

## Pipes older than the people using them

The Waterworks Bureau says the rise is needed to replace pipes laid during the city's rapid growth in the 1970s and early 1980s. Of the 1,620 km of water mains under Harukawa's streets, 31%, or about 500 km, are more than 40 years old, the legal service life for cast-iron and steel mains.

The bureau plans to spend ¥64 billion over the next 20 years on replacement and earthquake-proofing, doubling the pace of work from about 13 km of mains a year to 25 km. At the current pace, it would take almost 40 years just to replace the pipes that are already past their service life.

Pipe bursts are rising. The bureau recorded 46 bursts in fiscal 2025, up from 29 five years earlier. In February, a burst main under Otemachi-dori cut water to 2,100 households for 14 hours.

> We are not raising prices to balance the books this year. We are raising them so that in 2040 the water still comes out of the tap. -- Hiroshi Nagai, director of the Harukawa City Waterworks Bureau

## Fewer people, less water

At the same time, revenue is falling. Water use in the city has dropped by 9% since 2015 as the population shrinks and households install water-saving appliances, and the bureau's income from charges fell to ¥11.8 billion in fiscal 2025. Without a rise, the bureau says its reserve fund would be used up by fiscal 2028.

The bureau considered a two-stage rise of 5% in 2027 and 5% in 2029, but rejected it because it would cost more in total. A rise of 8.5% is expected to keep charges unchanged until at least 2031.

## What stays the same

Households receiving public assistance will remain exempt from the basic charge, and the reduced rate for households with a family member who needs home dialysis will continue. The bureau will send every customer a leaflet with the new rates in January, and a bill calculator will be added to its website.

!! What happens next
- 18 September: construction and waterworks committee approves the plan, 7–3
- 30 September: vote at the city council plenary session
- January 2027: leaflets with the new rates sent to all customers
- 1 April 2027: new charges take effect (first bill under the new tariff in June 2027)

## Opposition says timing is wrong

The three committee members who voted against belong to the Kagami Citizens' Network. Councillor Emi Tsuchiya said the bureau should first spend part of its reserve and seek more national funding for pipe replacement.

> Prices of food, electricity and petrol are going up. Asking households for 8.5% more on top of that, with no plan to protect pensioners above the welfare line, is the wrong decision at the wrong time. -- Emi Tsuchiya, Kagami Citizens' Network

Michiko Sawada, the former prefectural assembly member who has announced she will run for mayor in November, said she would "review" the rise if elected. Mayor Takuya Hirose, who is seeking a third term, defended the plan at a press conference on Friday, saying that "putting off the bill only makes it larger for our children".

Harukawa's charges are in the middle of the range for cities of its size in western Japan. After the rise, a two-person household would still pay about ¥300 less every two months than in the prefectural capital, the bureau says.
