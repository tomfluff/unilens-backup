Minori Prefecture declared the start of this winter's influenza season on Thursday 24 September, about six weeks earlier than last year, after the number of patients reported by its sentinel clinics passed the threshold of 1.0 per clinic.

In week 38 (14 to 20 September), the prefecture's sentinel clinics reported an average of 1.2 flu patients each. A preliminary count for the current week, released by the Minori Prefecture Infectious Disease Surveillance Centre on Friday morning, put the figure at 1.8. At the same point last year it was 0.3, and the season was not declared until early November.

[[chart:flu-weekly]]

Two classes at elementary schools in Harukawa were closed this week after several pupils came down with flu, the city board of education said. Most cases so far have been type A.

> We usually see the first school closures in November. Seeing them in September tells us not to wait: if you are eligible, book your vaccination now. -- Dr Akiko Noguchi, director of the Harukawa City Public Health Centre

## Who pays what

Harukawa's subsidised vaccination programme runs from 1 October 2026 to 31 January 2027 at 212 clinics across the city.

Residents aged 65 and over pay **¥1,500** for one dose. It is free for those in households receiving public assistance or exempt from resident tax, who must bring a certificate from the city. People aged 60 to 64 with a grade 1 heart, kidney, respiratory or immune disability are also eligible at ¥1,500 if they bring their disability certificate.

For children, the city has doubled its subsidy. Children aged six months to 12 years need two doses, two to four weeks apart, and the city will now pay ¥2,000 towards each dose, up from ¥1,000 last season. Teenagers aged 13 to 18, who need only one dose, receive a ¥2,000 subsidy for the first time. Families pay the rest of the clinic's price, which in Harukawa is usually between ¥3,500 and ¥4,500 a dose, so most will pay ¥1,500 to ¥2,500 after the subsidy.

The same subsidy applies to the nasal spray vaccine for children aged 2 to 18, which is given as a single spray in each nostril instead of an injection. It is offered at 34 clinics only, and supplies are limited.

The expanded children's subsidy will cost the city about ¥86 million this season, funded from the fiscal 2026 budget approved in March.

## Book early, bring your cards

Most participating clinics take bookings by phone or online, and the public health centre expects the first two weeks of October to be busy. The list of clinics, with those offering the nasal spray marked, is on the city's website and available at the public health centre in Otemachi and at branch offices.

!! What to bring
- Health insurance card or My Number card
- For children: the Maternal and Child Health Handbook, with the vaccination record
- For free vaccination: the public assistance or resident-tax exemption certificate
- For ages 60–64: the disability certificate
- The pre-vaccination questionnaire, filled in (available at clinics)

Vaccine makers ship in batches through the autumn, and the city says it has been told supply will be about the same as last season. Dr Noguchi advised people not to wait for December: protection takes about two weeks to build up after the injection.

Residents aged 65 and over can also receive this season's routine COVID-19 vaccination from 1 October, at a cost of ¥3,000. Most clinics can give both vaccines on the same day.

## If your child has flu

Children diagnosed with flu must stay away from school until five days have passed since the symptoms began and two days have passed since the fever went down (three days for preschool children). The city reminds parents that its emergency clinic will stay open later for children from 1 October: see our report on the [new night clinic hours](pediatric-night-clinic).

> Hand-washing, fresh air and staying home with a fever still do a lot. The vaccine does not stop every infection, but it makes serious illness much less likely, especially for older people and small children. -- Dr Noguchi
