Typhoon No. 21 is expected to pass close to Harukawa in the early hours of Monday 5 October. It is likely to bring the strongest winds the city has seen in several years, heavy rain across Minori Prefecture and a storm surge along Minori Bay.

At 15:00 on Saturday the typhoon was about 650 km south-south-west of Harukawa, according to the Minori Local Meteorological Observatory. Its central pressure was 960 hPa, with sustained winds of up to 35 m/s and gusts of up to 50 m/s. It was moving slowly north at about 10 km/h, but the observatory expects it to turn north-north-east on Sunday and speed up, keeping its strength as it crosses warm sea.

## Closest at about 04:00 on Monday

In the observatory's central forecast the centre comes within about 30 km of Harukawa at around 04:00 on Monday, passing to the west of the city before moving inland and weakening during the day. The forecast circle for Monday morning is still about 200 km across, so the track may yet shift.

A track to the west matters. A typhoon's strongest winds are on its eastern side, where its own winds add to the speed at which it is moving.

> A track just to the west puts the whole city on the dangerous side of the storm. Prepare on Saturday and on Sunday morning, not on Sunday night. -- Yumi Kaneko, forecaster, Minori Local Meteorological Observatory

## Wind, rain and the tide

Rain is expected from Sunday morning and should become heavy by Sunday evening. In the 24 hours to Monday evening the observatory expects 150–200 mm in the city and up to 250 mm in the mountains around Mount Tsukimi, with up to 40 mm in an hour at the peak. Mean winds could reach 25–30 m/s, with gusts of 40–45 m/s, strongest between midnight and 06:00 on Monday. Waves of up to 6 m are forecast in Minori Bay.

The timing is the main worry along the bay. High tide on Monday is at 05:12, about an hour after the typhoon's closest approach. The observatory says the sea could rise more than 2 m above its standard level along the coast at Minatomachi, Wangancho and Shiohama.

Storm, heavy rain and high wave warnings are likely to be issued on Sunday afternoon, and a storm surge warning on Sunday evening. Check the latest on our [weather page](page:weather).

## The Kagami River

Rain that falls in the mountains reaches the lower Kagami River six to nine hours later. The prefecture watches the gauge at Nishiki Bridge, where 3.2 m is the flood watch level, 4.1 m the level at which the city considers evacuation, and 4.6 m the danger level. In the September 2021 flood the gauge reached a record 4.9 m and the river overtopped its bank at Shimo-Kawabata, flooding 1,140 homes.

Since then the left bank has been raised by 1.0 m along the 1.1 km between Asahi Bridge and Minato Bridge. Work on the second section, down to the river mouth, began only on Thursday, and the stretch through the city centre, between Nishiki Bridge and Asahi Bridge, is not due to be strengthened until 2028 ([our report on the works](embankment-works)).

## What the city and HaruTetsu plan

The city's Disaster Management Office said it was likely to issue a Level 3 alert on Sunday afternoon, asking older people and anyone who needs help to move to go to a shelter, and that shelters would open at the same time. If the river or the tide rises faster than forecast, a Level 4 evacuation instruction for low-lying districts could follow on Sunday evening.

Sandbags are available free at City Hall and the five branch offices until 17:00 on Saturday and from 9:00 to 15:00 on Sunday.

Harukawa Railway (HaruTetsu) said it was considering stopping trains on Sunday evening and would announce its decision at 15:00 on Sunday. Until then, trains run to the weekend timetable. Ferries from Minori Port are likely to be cancelled from Sunday morning.

Lantern Festival organisers will take down the 4,000 lanterns already hung along the river on Sunday morning. The festival itself, on 10 and 11 October, is [not expected to be affected](festival-road-closures): the forecast for next Saturday is sunny, with a high of 23 °C.

!! Before the storm
- Bring in or tie down anything on balconies and in gardens
- Keep three days of drinking water and food; charge phones and power banks
- Check the city hazard map for your address and the way to your nearest shelter
- Keep medicine, documents and a torch in one bag
- Do not go to look at the river or the sea during the storm
