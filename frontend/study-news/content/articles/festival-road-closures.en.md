Drivers in central Harukawa face the city's largest road closures of the year next weekend, when the 68th Harukawa Lantern Festival returns to the banks of the Kagami River on Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October.

The Harukawa Lantern Festival Executive Committee and Harukawa Central Police Station on Friday published the traffic plan for the two days. Six roads will close or be restricted, the longest closure being Riverside Avenue, which shuts to all vehicles, bicycles included, between Nishiki Bridge and Asahi Bridge from 14:00 to 23:00 on both days.

The committee expects about 320,000 visitors, up from 304,000 last year, as the number of lanterns rises to 12,000 from 11,000. The programme is the same on both days. Stalls open along the 1.3 km riverside promenade at 16:00 and the lanterns in Castle Park, on Komachi-dori and along the river are lit from 17:30. At 18:00 a lantern procession leaves Harukawa Castle and makes its way down to the river at Nishiki Bridge by 20:30. From 19:30 to 21:00, lanterns are floated on the river from the riverside steps below Nishiki Bridge, beside the main stage near Shiromachi Station. The festival closes at 21:00.

## Which roads close, and when

Komachi-dori, the old-town street that runs 650 metres from the Castle Park east gate to the North Exit of Harukawa Central Station, becomes pedestrian-only from 12:00 to 22:00 on both days. Shops on the street can take deliveries until 11:30.

Nishiki Bridge closes to vehicles from 16:00 to 22:00 each day. From 19:00 to 21:00 on both evenings, walkers on the bridge will only be able to cross from north to south, to keep people moving during the lantern floating below.

Asahi Bridge stays open but is reduced to a single northbound lane from 15:00 to 23:00. Otemachi-dori, between the Castle Park south gate and Nishiki Bridge, closes from 17:30 to 21:00 on both days for the lantern procession, which runs from 18:00 to 20:30. The road in front of Shiromachi Station, from the station square at the River Exit down to Riverside Avenue, is closed to vehicles from 17:00 to 22:00.

## No parking: use the park-and-ride

There will be no festival car parks in the centre, and the police say cars stopping on Riverside Avenue before 14:00 will be towed. Instead, two park-and-ride sites open from 15:00: the Harukawa University Kita Campus car park, with 1,200 spaces, and Minori Port Car Park No. 2, with 800. Both charge ¥500 per car, which covers the shuttle bus for everyone in it. Shuttles run every 10 minutes from 15:00 to 22:30 to the Castle Park east gate.

Cyclists can use a temporary bicycle park for 1,500 bikes in the grounds of Shiroyama Elementary School, a ten-minute walk from the riverside. Harukawa City runs a wheelchair viewing area near the North Exit of Harukawa Central Station; places must be booked with the city in advance.

City buses on routes 3, 5 and 12 will be diverted away from the river from 14:00 to 23:00 on both days. The city transport bureau says delays of up to 30 minutes are likely on other routes that cross the centre in the evening.

## Ten extra trains each evening

Harukawa Railway (HaruTetsu) will run ten extra trains on both days, on top of its Saturday and holiday timetable. All of them stop at every station, and none appears in the station timetables or in HaruTetsu's journey planner.

On the Kagami Line, two extra trains bring visitors in from Kamano, leaving at 17:20 and 18:20 and reaching Harukawa Central at 17:43 and 18:43. Three late trains take them home: the 21:40 from Harukawa Central to Kamano (arriving 22:03), the 22:30 to Kagami-kyō (23:09) and the 23:25 to Kamano (23:48). Each leaves Shiromachi three minutes after Harukawa Central. On the Bayside Line, five extra trains leave Harukawa Central for Minori Port at 21:15, 21:45, 22:15, 22:45 and 23:30; the last arrives at 23:58.

The regular last trains from Harukawa Central on both nights are the 22:15 to Tsukimi Onsen and the 22:50 to Kamano on the Kagami Line, and the 23:00 to Minori Port and the 23:35 to Minori-kōen on the Bayside Line.

Shiromachi, four minutes from the main stage by the River Exit, is expected to be the busiest station. HaruTetsu says it may restrict entry there from 18:00 to 21:30, asking passengers to wait outside, and the station's coin lockers will be closed from 15:00. Limited express Tsukikage 21, 24, 25 and 28 will not stop at Shiromachi on either day. Harukawa Central is a 20-minute walk along the river, and its North Exit is five minutes from the Asahi Bridge end of the promenade.

The Tsukikage peak surcharge of ¥200 applies from 10 to 12 October, and the Tsukimi Onsen Yukemuri Ticket cannot be used on those days. HaruTetsu advises visitors to charge their HaruCa cards or buy return tickets before the evening: ticket machines at Harukawa Central had queues of up to 40 minutes after the festival closed last year. (HaruTetsu's [new timetable](railway-timetable) starts on 14 November and does not affect the festival.)

!! Festival at a glance
- Dates: Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October, the same programme on both days; stalls from 16:00, lanterns lit from 17:30, closing at 21:00
- Lantern procession: 18:00–20:30, from Harukawa Castle along Otemachi-dori to Nishiki Bridge
- Lantern floating: 19:30–21:00, Nishiki Bridge riverside steps by the main stage (3,000 floating lanterns, 1,500 each evening, ¥1,000, on sale until 9 October)
- Nearest stations: Shiromachi (River Exit, 4 minutes; entry may be restricted 18:00–21:30) and Harukawa Central (North Exit, 5 minutes)
- Park-and-ride: ¥500 per car; shuttles every 10 minutes, 15:00–22:30

## Crowd sensors and 620 stewards

After Nishiki Bridge became gridlocked for about 25 minutes during the floating lantern release last year, when 14 people were treated for minor injuries, the committee has doubled the number of stewards on the bridge. In all, 620 stewards and 180 police officers will be on duty, and 46 sensors on lamp posts will measure crowd density in real time as part of a [trial with Harukawa University](crowd-sensors). Drones are banned over the festival area.

There are 140 registered food and craft stalls along the promenade, open from 16:00. Unregistered stalls will be asked to leave.

## What about the typhoon?

[Typhoon No. 21](typhoon-forecast) is expected to pass close to Harukawa early on Monday 5 October. The committee said the 4,000 lanterns already hung along the riverside would be taken down on Sunday morning and put back between 6 and 9 October.

> The festival is five days after the typhoon. Unless the riverside is damaged, we expect to go ahead as planned. -- Haruo Tanabe

The committee will announce on Thursday 8 October at 15:00 whether every event, including the lantern floating, goes ahead. Updates will appear on the festival website and in our [live coverage of the typhoon](page:live).
