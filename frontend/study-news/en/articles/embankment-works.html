Embankment works move downstream: 1.2 km of riverside path to close from 1 October

The second phase of Harvest Prefecture's ¥8.6 billion project to raise the Mirror River's left bank starts next Thursday. Walkers and cyclists will be diverted onto Minato-dori until about March 2028, and up to 140 lorries a day will use the road.

M
By Mariko FujisawaCity affairs reporter
Published Thu 24 Sep 2026, 17:20 JST
Share this article
3 min read

Five years after the Mirror River came over its banks at Shimo-Kawabata, the work to make sure it does not happen again is moving towards the sea.

Harvest Prefecture said on Thursday that the second phase of its embankment works on the river's left bank will begin on 1 October, covering the 1.2 km between Minato Bridge and the river mouth. The riverside walking and cycling path along that stretch closes the same day and is not expected to reopen until about March 2028.

The project, which began in April 2024, raises the crest of the embankment by 1.0 metre to 7.4 metres, drives steel sheet piles into the bank to stop water seeping through it, and adds a 12-metre-wide berm, a terrace on the land side that makes the bank harder to wash away. In all, 3.2 km of bank will be rebuilt by March 2029 at a cost of ¥8.6 billion.

Why the left bank

In September 2021, heavy rain upstream pushed the water at the Nishiki Bridge gauge to 4.9 metres, the highest since records began and well above the 4.6-metre danger level. The river overtopped the bank at Shimo-Kawabata and 1,140 homes were flooded, most of them in low-lying streets built on former rice paddies in the 1960s and 1970s.

The first phase, between Asahi Bridge and Minato Bridge, rebuilt the stretch where the water came over. It was finished in March, on budget, at ¥2.9 billion.

Phase 1 closed the gap where the river came through in 2021. But a bank is only as strong as its lowest point, and the lowest point is now downstream.

Satoshi Kudo, head of the Harvest Prefecture Mirror River Office

Phase 2 costs ¥3.5 billion and will be carried out by Mirror Minato Construction JV, a joint venture of two local firms. The third and last phase, between Nishiki Bridge and Asahi Bridge, is the stretch of riverside used by the Lantern Festival. It is planned for April 2028 to March 2029, so that only one festival, in 2028, is affected.

Path closed, lorries on Minato-dori

From 1 October the path between Minato Bridge and the river mouth will be fenced off. Walkers and cyclists will be signed onto Minato-dori, which runs parallel to the river about 200 metres inland; the detour adds about 400 metres.

Lorries carrying soil and steel will use Minato-dori on weekdays from 8:30 to 17:00, with up to 140 trips a day at the busiest stage next spring. There will be no night or weekend work, except in emergencies. The sheet piles will be pressed into the ground hydraulically rather than hammered, which the prefecture says will keep noise below 70 decibels at the nearest houses.

At a briefing for residents at the Minatomachi community hall on 19 September, attended by about 120 people, several asked about safety on Minato-dori, which is also the route to Minatomachi Junior High School. The contractor said it would post guards at the two school crossings from 7:30 to 8:30 and from 15:00 to 17:00 on school days.

I have walked my dog along the river every morning for 30 years. Eighteen months on Minato-dori with lorries is a long time. But I was here in 2021. I will take the lorries.

Toshio Yamane, 74, Minatomachi resident

The closure also changes the course of the Springvale Castle Marathon in February, which will use Minato-dori between km 14 and km 18.

A pump for the water that falls inside

Raising the bank deals only with water coming from the river. In 2021 part of Shimo-Kawabata also flooded because rain falling behind the bank could not drain into a river that was already full. The city has proposed ¥1.1 billion in its September supplementary budget for a drainage pump station at Shimo-Kawabata that would lift 12 cubic metres of water a second over the bank. If the council approves the budget in its vote on 30 September, design work will start this autumn, with completion planned for fiscal 2028.

Mirror River water levels at the Nishiki Bridge gauge

  • Flood watch level: 3.2 m
  • Evacuation decision level: 4.1 m
  • Danger level: 4.6 m
  • Record: 4.9 m (September 2021)

Readings from the Nishiki Bridge gauge are published every ten minutes on the prefecture's river information website. The prefecture is asking residents near the river to check the city's flood hazard map, which was updated in April to reflect the Phase 1 works.
