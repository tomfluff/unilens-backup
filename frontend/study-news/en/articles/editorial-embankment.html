Editorial: The river will not wait until 2029

The stretch of the Kagami River embankment that runs through the heart of Harukawa is the last in line to be strengthened. The prefecture and the city should bring it forward, and every candidate for mayor should say how.

E
By Editorial boardThe Harukawa Herald
Published Sat 3 Oct 2026, 06:00 JST
Share this article
3 min read

Five years ago last month, the Kagami River rose to 4.9 m at the Nishiki Bridge gauge, the highest level ever recorded there. It overtopped its bank at Shimo-Kawabata and flooded 1,140 homes. Many families in that district spent the following winter in temporary housing; some never returned.

Since then, Minori Prefecture has done much of what it promised. The first phase of its ¥8.6 billion programme to strengthen 3.2 km of the left bank, the 1.1 km between Asahi Bridge and Minato Bridge, was completed in March, on time and within its ¥2.9 billion budget. The crest is now 1.0 m higher, at 7.4 m, backed by steel sheet piles and a 12 m berm. The second phase, from Minato Bridge to the river mouth, began on Thursday. It will cost ¥3.5 billion and close the riverside path there until about March 2028. Walkers, cyclists and the runners of next February's Castle Marathon will have to take Minato-dori instead. That is a price worth paying.

The wrong way round

The problem is the order. The third phase, the 0.9 km between Nishiki Bridge and Asahi Bridge, is not due to start until April 2028, and will not be finished until March 2029. This is the stretch that runs past the old town, Castle Park and the riverside where 300,000 people will gather for the Lantern Festival next weekend. City Hall is a few hundred metres away. It is, in short, the stretch where a breach would do the most harm.

The prefecture's Kagami River Office gives three reasons for the timetable: the contractor can work on only one section at a time; the trucks for both sections would have to share narrow roads; and national subsidies are spread over several years. We do not dismiss these. But none of them prevents the prefecture from starting the survey and design work for Phase 3 now, so that construction can begin the moment Phase 2 allows. Nor do they rule out strengthening the most vulnerable few hundred metres of Phase 3 first, during the dry winter months of 2027.

The city's part

The city has its own work to do. This week the council approved ¥1.1 billion in the September supplementary budget for a drainage pump station at Shimo-Kawabata, able to pump 12 cubic metres a second out of the low-lying streets into the river. That is welcome. But the pumps will not be ready until fiscal 2028, and a pump station behind a weak embankment protects only half of what it should.

Harukawa is not a rich city. Its debt stands at ¥214.5 billion, or ¥519,600 for every resident, and the cost of the new library is still being paid off. We understand why the mayor and the council are cautious. But the city could offer the prefecture something concrete: a share of the design costs for Phase 3, or the use of city land for a second construction yard so that two sections can be worked at once. Money spent before a flood buys far more than money spent after one.

A question for the candidates

The mayoral election on 8 November gives voters a chance to ask. In our poll last week, 34% of respondents named disaster prevention and flood control as the most important issue for their vote, ahead of the cost of living at 27% (poll report). All three candidates say they will keep the city safe. Only one, Mayor Takuya Hirose, makes the embankment and the pump station a headline pledge, and even he has not said whether he would press the prefecture to change its timetable.

We ask each candidate to answer two questions before our debate at Harukawa Civic Hall on 22 October. In what year will the embankment through the city centre be finished? And what will the city pay to make that happen?

As this editorial goes to press, Typhoon No. 21 is moving north towards Minori Prefecture, and the observatory expects it to pass close to the city early on Monday (forecast). We hope the river stays within its banks. But hope, and luck, are not a flood defence. The next flood will not check the prefecture's timetable first.
