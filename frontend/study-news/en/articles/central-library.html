New Central Library to open on 7 November with 480,000 books and 640 seats

The ¥6.2 billion library next to Harukawa Central Station will have more than three times as many seats as the 1974 building at Shiroyama, which closes on 18 October. There will be no lending from the central library for three weeks during the move.

M
By Mariko FujisawaCity affairs reporter
Published Sat 26 Sep 2026, 12:00 JSTUpdated Mon 28 Sep 2026, 10:30 JST
Share this article
3 min read Comments

Harukawa's new Central Library will open on Saturday 7 November next to the South Exit of Harukawa Central Station, the city Board of Education said on Friday, ending nearly a decade of planning and three years of construction.

The five-storey building, with 9,800 square metres of floor space, replaces the Central Library at Shiroyama, which opened in 1974 and has long been too small for its collection: about a third of its books sit in closed stacks that readers must request at the counter.

The new library can hold 620,000 volumes and will open with 480,000, about 410,000 moved from Shiroyama and 70,000 bought new. It has 640 seats, against 180 in the old building.

Floor by floor

  • Ground floor: café, new books and magazines, returns desk, and a 24-hour return box at the east entrance
  • 2nd floor: children's library, with a story-time room and space for pushchairs
  • 3rd floor: general collection and reading area
  • 4th floor: local history collection and 12 study rooms that can be booked online
  • 5th floor: rooftop terrace with views of Harukawa Castle and Mount Tsukimi

Self-service machines will let readers borrow books without queuing, and reserved books can be collected from lockers until 21:00.

For fifty years people have told us the library is where they go when they have nowhere else to go. Now we can give them a seat.

Naoko Ishida, director of the Central Library

Opening hours

The library will stay open until 21:00 from Tuesday to Friday, two hours later than the Shiroyama building, and will close on Mondays.

The move: three weeks without lending

The Shiroyama library will close for good at the end of Sunday 18 October. From 19 October to 6 November there will be no lending from the central library while about 410,000 books are packed and moved. Books borrowed from 1 September onwards are all due back on 21 November, so nobody needs to return anything during the move. Books can still be returned to the six branch libraries, which stay open as usual, or to the return box at Shiroyama until 18 October.

Library cards remain valid in the new building, and readers do not need to register again. Reservations made before 18 October will be kept.

What it costs

The building cost ¥6.2 billion: ¥5.4 billion for construction and ¥0.8 billion for fittings and furniture. This year's city budget puts ¥3.6 billion towards construction, the final payment, and ¥2.05 billion towards library services, up from ¥1.42 billion last year. The city has also proposed ¥0.6 billion in its September supplementary budget, which the council votes on on 30 September, for new books, computer systems and the move.

Running the new library will cost about ¥1.31 billion a year, against ¥820 million for the old one, mainly because of the longer opening hours and 28 additional staff. Some councillors have questioned the running costs at a time when the city plans to raise water charges; the Board of Education expects visits to double to about 900,000 a year.

What happens to Shiroyama?

The future of the 1974 building, designed by a local architect and admired by some for its concrete reading hall, has not been decided. The city will decide by March 2027 whether to demolish it, sell it or find it a new use. A group of residents has asked for it to be kept as a community space.

The new library has 40 parking spaces, most of them reserved for disabled visitors, and the city is asking readers to come by train, bus or bicycle. There is a bicycle park for 300 bikes under the building.

Key dates

  • 18 October (Sun): last day at the Shiroyama library
  • 19 October – 6 November: no lending from the central library; branches open as usual
  • 7 November (Sat): new Central Library opens
  • 21 November: due date for all books borrowed from 1 September
Correction (Mon 28 Sep 2026, 10:30 JST) An earlier version of this article said the new Central Library would open on 3 November. It opens on Saturday 7 November 2026.
