Ferry Port container traffic rebounds 6% as new Busan route and third crane take effect

The port handled 131,900 containers in the first eight months of 2026, recovering from a fall last fiscal year. Growth has picked up since a weekly service to Busan and Kaohsiung began in June.

Y
By Yui HamadaBusiness and transport reporter
Published Thu 1 Oct 2026, 16:30 JST
Share this article
2 min read

Container traffic at Ferry Port rose 6.1% in the first eight months of 2026, recovering from a decline last year, according to figures published on Thursday by the Harvest Prefecture Port Authority.

The port handled 131,900 TEU (twenty-foot equivalent units, the standard measure of container traffic) from January to August, up from 124,300 in the same period of 2025. In fiscal 2025, which ended in March, traffic fell 4.2% to 186,400 TEU from 194,600, after a shipping line ended its weekly service to Shanghai in May last year.

Every month this year has been busier than the same month of 2025, but the gap widened after June, when a new weekly service linking Ferry Port with Busan in South Korea and Kaohsiung in Taiwan began. From January to May traffic was up 5.0%; from June to August it was up 7.8%. July, at 17,900 TEU, was the busiest month in the port's history.

Busan gives our shippers a connection to the whole world in one stop. Before June, most of them sent containers by truck to Kobe or Hakata.

Kazuya Ota, director of the Harvest Prefecture Port Authority

A third crane

The port's third gantry crane went into service in September, after two years of construction work on the quay. The port authority says it will allow a container ship to be unloaded and loaded in about 14 hours instead of 20, and that it has already persuaded one more shipping line to consider calling at Springvale from next spring.

Exports through the port include auto parts from factories in the Mirror valley, frozen fish and processed yuzu products, and a growing share of Springvale ware tableware, which reached a record ¥2.84 billion in exports last year. Imports are dominated by animal feed, building materials and furniture.

Trucks and neighbours

The growth has a cost for residents of Wangancho and Tidewater, the districts closest to the terminal. Truck traffic on the port access road rose by about 12% in the first half of the year, and residents' associations have asked the prefecture for a second access route.
