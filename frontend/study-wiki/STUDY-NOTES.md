# Openpedia: candidate study tasks

Reading and looking things up. Every answer is on the site; most need two or more places (an article and its infobox, a table, a chart shown as an image without useful alt text, a footnote, a second article). "Today" on the site is early October 2026 (the main page's "On this day" is 4 October). Paths are under `en/` and `ja/` (same paths in both languages).

## 1. How old is the castle keep?

- EN: "How old is the keep of Harukawa Castle? Is it the original one?"
- JA: 「春川城の天守は築何年ですか？建てられた当時のものですか？」
- Where: `wiki/harukawa-castle.html` (lead, History, the buildings table, Keep), `wiki/history-of-harukawa.html` (timeline).
- Why: several "ages" compete (stone walls 1603–1611, first keep 1611 or 1612 per a footnote, second keep 1755, reconstruction 1966, Tatsumi Turret 1609), and the answer depends on today's date.
- Answer: Not original. The present keep is a reinforced-concrete reconstruction that opened on 3 November 1966, so it is 59 years old and reaches its 60th anniversary next month. The first keep (1611; 1612 in some sources) burned in 1749, and its 1755 replacement was demolished in 1874. The oldest surviving building is the Tatsumi Turret of 1609 (417 years old, 357 years older than the keep).

## 2. Wettest month

- EN: "Which month gets the most rain in Harukawa, and by how much compared with the next wettest?"
- JA: 「春川で一番雨が多いのは何月ですか？2番目に多い月とどのくらい違いますか？」
- Where: `wiki/climate-of-harukawa.html` (climate table, chart image with no useful alt, a footnote), `wiki/harukawa.html` (Climate).
- Why: needs reading a wide table or a chart, a subtraction, and a footnote that contradicts older guidebooks.
- Answer: June, 312.5 mm, which is 22.8 mm more than September (289.7 mm). On the older 1961–1990 normals September was wettest (about 301 mm against 287 mm), which is why older guidebooks say September. June has about six times the rain of January (52.4 mm), the driest month.

## 3. Who taught whom

- EN: "Who taught the potter Hanae Ishizuka, and who carries on her kiln today?"
- JA: 「陶芸家の石塚花枝は誰に師事しましたか？今、彼女の窯を継いでいるのは誰ですか？」
- Where: `wiki/hanae-ishizuka.html`, `wiki/harukawa-ware.html` (Notable potters table: "Trained under"; Kilns table).
- Why: the lineage spans two articles and a table; one name (Kawabe Shūzan) is a red link with no article.
- Answer: She trained under Kawabe Shūzan from 1938, the first woman he accepted. Her student Ren Mizuhara (apprenticed 1975–1984) has headed her Kasumi Kiln since 2009; Mizuhara in turn trained Mio Takano (2010–2017), who founded the Tsubame Kiln in 2018.

## 4. Fastest train to the hot spring and what it costs

- EN: "What is the fastest way from Harukawa Central to Tsukimi Onsen by train, and how much is it for one adult?"
- JA: 「春川中央から月見温泉まで一番速い電車は？大人1人いくらですか？」
- Where: `wiki/tsukikage.html` (stops, surcharge, timetable revision), `wiki/harukawa-railway.html` (fares table), `wiki/kagami-line.html` (station table, Services).
- Why: fare and surcharge are in different places and must be added; the surcharge depends on distance; a timetable change is coming; the festival weekend is a peak date.
- Answer: The limited express Tsukikage: 39 minutes, stopping at Shiromachi, Kamano and Kagami-kyō (rapids take 55 minutes, locals 63). From 14 November 2026 it also stops at Tsukimi-guchi and takes 41 minutes. Fare ¥730 (¥715 with HaruCa) plus the surcharge for 26 km or more, ¥760, = ¥1,490; the Premium car costs ¥700 more (¥2,190); on peak dates, including 10–12 October (the festival weekend), add ¥200. All seats are reserved. (These figures agree with the railway site, `study-transit`.)

## 5. The Lantern Festival this year

- EN: "When is the Lantern Festival this year, and when are the floating lanterns released? Can I float one myself?"
- JA: 「今年の春川灯籠まつりはいつですか？灯籠流しは何時から？自分でも流せますか？」
- Where: `wiki/harukawa-lantern-festival.html` (lead, programme table, Floating lanterns), main page (In the news).
- Why: date and times are in a table; the edition number differs by source (footnote).
- Answer: 10–11 October 2026 (Saturday–Sunday). On Saturday 18:00–20:30 about 12,000 lanterns are released just below Kagami Bridge and drift 1.4 km to Shiomi Bridge. Anyone can dedicate one for ¥800, online from 1 September or at riverbank stalls until 17:00 on the Saturday (they usually sell out by mid-afternoon). It is the 282nd festival by the committee's count, the 281st by the Harukawa Nippō's.

## 6. Highest mountain

- EN: "Is Mount Tsukimi the highest mountain in Minori Prefecture? How high is it?"
- JA: 「月見山はみのり県で一番高い山ですか？標高は？」
- Where: `wiki/mount-tsukimi.html` (lead, footnote), `wiki/minori-prefecture.html` (infobox, Geography), the locator map image.
- Why: the obvious guess is wrong; the height has a footnoted revision.
- Answer: No. Mount Hiwada (1,477 m) on the northern border is the highest; Mount Tsukimi is 1,214 m (second highest). Older maps and signs say 1,216 m; a 2014 survey revised it.

## 7. Population peak

- EN: "When did Harukawa have the most people, and why did the population jump in the early 1950s?"
- JA: 「春川市の人口が最も多かったのはいつですか？1950年代前半に人口が急に増えたのはなぜですか？」
- Where: `wiki/harukawa.html` (Demographics: population table and chart), `wiki/history-of-harukawa.html` (chart, Postwar), footnote on the 2025 figure.
- Why: chart without useful alt text; the cause of the jump is in another section; two current figures disagree.
- Answer: The peak was 431,072 at the 2005 census. From 171,346 (1950) to 238,517 (1955) because the city absorbed four villages (Kawamata, Ichinose, Uchiura, Sakurahama) on 1 October 1954. The 2025 estimate is 412,800; the city's own resident register counted 409,962 on 1 September 2026 (different method, footnote).

## 8. Largest part of the economy

- EN: "What is the biggest sector of Harukawa's economy, and roughly how much is it worth?"
- JA: 「春川市の経済で一番大きい産業は何ですか？金額にするとどのくらいですか？」
- Where: `wiki/economy-of-harukawa.html` (sector chart image, generated table, Industries).
- Why: chart vs table; a percentage to turn into yen.
- Answer: Manufacturing, 21.4 % of the ¥1.68 trillion gross city product (FY2022), about ¥360 billion, led by the Harukawa Shipyard (about 3,100 workers) and Kagami Precision (about 2,400).

## 9. Getting to the castle by train

- EN: "Which station is closest to the castle, and does the limited express stop there?"
- JA: 「お城に一番近い駅はどこですか？特急は止まりますか？」
- Where: `wiki/kagami-line.html` (station table with ● and ◇ marks explained only in the legend row), `wiki/harukawa-castle.html` (Visiting), `wiki/tsukikage.html` (stopping pattern), `wiki/harukawa-lantern-festival.html` (transport).
- Why: a wide table with symbols explained in its legend row, and an exception for the festival days.
- Answer: Shiromachi (K02, 1.8 km from Harukawa Central); the keep is about 8 minutes' walk uphill from its Castle Exit. Yes, the Tsukikage stops there (its stops are Shiromachi, Kamano and Kagami-kyō, plus Tsukimi-guchi from 14 November), except that Tsukikage 21, 24, 25 and 28 skip Shiromachi on the festival days, 10 and 11 October. From Harukawa Central the castle is a 15-minute walk or 6 minutes by City Bus 1.

## 10. The prefecture's name

- EN: "Why is Minori Prefecture written in hiragana and not in kanji?"
- JA: 「みのり県はなぜ漢字ではなくひらがなで書くのですか？」
- Where: `wiki/minori-prefecture.html` (Name), main page (Did you know).
- Why: a short explanation buried in history; the old kanji appears in several articles.
- Answer: The prefecture was founded on 14 November 1871 as 実野県 (after Minori Province, 実野国). Because 実野 was often misread ("Jitsuno", "Saneno"), the official name became the hiragana みのり県 on 1 April 1971, the centenary.

## 11. What to eat now

- EN: "I'm visiting Harukawa this week. Which local dishes are in season, and is the oyster hot pot one of them?"
- JA: 「今週春川に行きます。今が旬の郷土料理は？牡蠣鍋は食べられますか？」
- Where: `wiki/cuisine-of-harukawa.html` (dish table: Season column; one section per dish).
- Why: needs today's date and a comparison across table rows.
- Answer: In early October, Kagami-jiru (grilled-ayu clear soup with yuzu, June–October) is in its last weeks; tōrō manjū (lantern buns, all year, most sold at the festival) and onsen yu-soba (all year) are available. Tai-yuzu meshi's season is March–May and Minori kaki-nabe (oyster hot pot) is November–March, so not yet.

## 12. A novelist's age

- EN: "How old was Michiyo Tōno when she died, and what is her best-known book about?"
- JA: 「遠野美千代は何歳で亡くなりましたか？代表作はどんな話ですか？」
- Where: `wiki/michiyo-tono.html` (infobox, lead, footnote on the birth year, bibliography table), `wiki/harukawa-lantern-festival.html` (In culture).
- Why: two birth years (footnote); the answer needs a date calculation.
- Answer: 73. Born 4 August 1914 (family register; she gave 1915 in her memoir), died 19 November 1987 at Tsukimi Onsen. "The River Mirror" (1956) tells of the Great Genbun Flood of 1739 and the first lantern memorial through Osayo, a fisherman's daughter from Uomachi; it won the Kōyō Prize in 1957 and was filmed in 1960.

## 13. Family visit to the castle and museum

- EN: "How much would it cost two adults and one 10-year-old to go into the castle keep? Is there a combined ticket with the art museum?"
- JA: 「大人2人と10歳の子ども1人で天守に入るといくらですか？美術館との共通券はありますか？」
- Where: `wiki/harukawa-castle.html` (Visiting: admission table), `wiki/minori-prefectural-museum-of-art.html` (Visiting).

## 14. Worst yuzu year

- EN: "In the last ten years, which year had the smallest yuzu harvest in Minori, and why?"
- JA: 「ここ10年で、みのり県のユズの収穫量が一番少なかったのは何年ですか？理由は？」
- Where: `wiki/yuzu-cultivation-in-minori.html` (production chart image and table with notes).
- Why: chart with values only as small bar labels, reasons only in the table's notes column.
- Answer: 2016, 3,640 t, after a spring frost (2018 was also low, 3,710 t, after the July heavy rain). The record was 4,480 t in 2019; 2023 was 4,350 t, third in Japan.

## 15. How long is the river?

- EN: "How long is the Kagami River?"
- JA: 「鏡川の長さは何キロですか？」
- Where: `wiki/kagami-river.html` (infobox, footnote), mentioned in `wiki/kagami-line.html`.
- Why: two official figures; the reason is only in a note.
- Answer: 94 km by the national river authority's figure; the prefecture's register says 96 km because it counts the Hosoya headwater stream. Basin 1,620 km²; it rises on the north slope of Mount Tsukimi and enters Minori Bay at Harukawa.
