'''Minori Prefecture''' (みのり県, ''Minori-ken'') is a prefecture of Japan. Its capital and largest city is [[harukawa|Harukawa]], on [[minori-bay|Minori Bay]] at the mouth of the [[kagami-river|Kagami River]]. The prefecture had an estimated 1,086,400 inhabitants on 1 October 2025 and covers 4,512.8 km², giving a density of about 241 people per km².<ref name="popest">Minori Prefecture Statistics Division (2025). ''Population Estimates of Minori Prefecture, 1 October 2025''. Minori Prefecture, pp. 1–4.</ref> It corresponds to the former Minori Province and was ruled during the Edo period mainly by the [[asagiri-clan|Asagiri clan]] from [[harukawa-castle|Harukawa Castle]]. It is known for [[harukawa-ware|Harukawa ware]], yuzu, oysters and sea bream, shipbuilding, and the hot springs and mountain scenery of [[tsukimi-onsen|Tsukimi Onsen]] and [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Tsukimi]].

== Name ==
The prefecture takes its name from Minori Province (実野国), one of the provinces of ancient Japan. The name is usually connected with ''minori'' (実り), "ripening" or "harvest". When the prefecture was created in 1871 it was written with the province's characters as 実野県, but the reading ''Minori'' of 実野 is irregular, and the name was often misread as "Jitsuno" or "Saneno", even in official correspondence.<ref name="kenshi">Minori Prefecture (1972). ''One Hundred Years of Minori Prefecture''. Minori Prefecture, pp. 412–419.</ref>

To mark the prefecture's centenary, the official name was changed to the hiragana form みのり県 on 1 April 1971. The change followed a 1969 survey in which most residents favored the hiragana spelling, and because a prefecture's name can only be changed by law, it required an act of the National Diet, passed in 1970.{{efn|The province, Minori Province, is still written 実野国 in historical works, and the old form 実野県 appears in documents and on buildings from before 1971.}} The prefecture is thus one of the few in Japan whose name is written in hiragana rather than kanji.

== History ==
{{see|history-of-harukawa|asagiri-clan}}
Minori Province was established in the 8th century; its districts included Kagami District, named after the river. From the 1400s to 1600 the south of the province was ruled by the Uchiura clan from Kurayama Castle. After the Battle of Sekigahara (1600), Asagiri Tadamasa received the province with an assessed yield of 186,000 ''koku'' and in 1603 began Harukawa Castle and its castle town. In 1651 a branch family, the Uchiura Asagiri, received 10,000 ''koku'', forming the small Uchiura Domain.<ref name="uchida">Uchida, Masao (2004). ''The Asagiri and Their Castle Town''. Harukawa University Press, pp. 41–58.</ref>

When the domains were abolished in 1871, the Harukawa Domain became Harukawa Prefecture. On 14 November 1871 it was merged with Uchiura Prefecture, the former branch domain, and Kitano Prefecture, formed from former shogunate lands in the north, to create 実野県, with Harukawa as its capital. In the 20th century the prefecture developed shipbuilding and machinery around Harukawa; the city was badly damaged in an air raid on 29 July 1945. The Tsukimi–Kagami Prefectural Natural Park was designated in 1959, and in 1971 the prefecture adopted its present name and opened Minori Airport. Mergers between 2003 and 2006 reduced the number of municipalities from 41 to the present 22.{{cn}}

== Geography ==
[[File:mountain-01.jpg|[[mount-tsukimi|Mount Tsukimi]] (1,214 m), the best-known mountain in the prefecture, seen from the town of Kagami]]

The north of the prefecture is mountainous. The highest point is Mount Hiwada (日和田山, 1,477 m) on the northern border; Mount Tsukimi (1,214 m), a dormant volcano about 32 km north-east of Harukawa, is the best known but only the second highest. The largest river, the Kagami River, rises on Mount Tsukimi and flows 94 km to Minori Bay; its basin of 1,620 km² covers about 36% of the prefecture.<ref name="office">Kagami River Office (2024). ''Outline of the Kagami River System''. National river administration, Harukawa, p. 2.</ref> The plains around Harukawa and Kitano hold most of the population.

The south of the prefecture faces Minori Bay (286 km²), entered between Cape Okitsu and the Uchiura Peninsula, with Kasumi Island and Sagi Island. The coast has a humid subtropical climate; Harukawa has an annual mean temperature of 16.5 °C (see [[climate-of-harukawa]]), while the mountains of the north are much cooler and receive snow in winter. The Tsukimi–Kagami Prefectural Natural Park (1959) covers Mount Tsukimi, the Tsukimi Valley and the Kagami Gorge.

The nine cities together have 852,800 inhabitants, about 78.5% of the prefecture. Kagami and Tsukimi, the two towns of the upper Kagami valley, form Kagami District.<ref name="popest"/>

== Economy ==
[[File:yuzu-01.jpg|Terraced yuzu orchard on the Uchiura Peninsula above Minori Bay]]

The prefecture's economy is centered on Harukawa, whose gross city product of ¥1.68 trillion (FY2022) is about two-fifths of the prefectural total (see [[economy-of-harukawa]]). The main industries are shipbuilding, at the Harukawa Shipyard of Minori Heavy Industries; machinery, led by Kagami Precision; and railway rolling stock, built by Nishiura Sharyō, which made the 8000 series of the [[tsukikage|Tsukikage]]. Harukawa ware, a nationally designated Traditional Craft since 1979, was worth ¥2.1 billion in 2024.

Minori is the third-largest producer of yuzu in Japan, with 4,350 tonnes in 2023 from about 520 ha (see [[yuzu-cultivation-in-minori]]). Minori Bay yields about 6,300 tonnes of shucked oysters a year from about 1,920 rafts, along with farmed sea bream. Minori Bank, founded in 1897, is the main regional bank.<ref name="keizai">Minori Prefecture Planning Division (2025). ''Minori Prefecture Economic Report 2025''. Minori Prefecture, pp. 10–24.</ref>

== Transport ==
The [[harukawa-railway|Harukawa Railway]] (HaruTetsu) runs the prefecture's main local railway: the [[kagami-line|Kagami Line]] (38.6 km, 14 stations) from [[harukawa-central-station|Harukawa Central Station]] up the Kagami valley to Tsukimi Onsen, and the [[bayside-line|Bayside Line]] (16.2 km, 9 stations) along the bay to Minori Port. Its contactless card is [[haruca|HaruCa]]. Minori Airport, opened in 1971 in Nishiura, 22 km south-west of Harukawa, handles domestic flights. The Minori Expressway crosses the prefecture, and the Hiwada Road Tunnel (1979) links the north with the neighboring prefecture. Ferries sail from Minori Port to Kasumi Island in 35 minutes.

== Culture ==
The best-known event is the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Harukawa Lantern Festival]], held since 1740 in memory of the victims of the 1739 flood and now on the second weekend of October. Harukawa ware is made mainly in Suyama, where the Kamano Pottery Fair is held in front of Kamano Station on the first weekend of May. Tsukimi washi, a handmade paper from the town of Tsukimi, is used for the festival's lanterns. Local dishes include ''tai-yuzu meshi'' (sea bream rice with yuzu) and Minori ''kaki-nabe'' (oyster hot pot) (see [[cuisine-of-harukawa]]). The [[minori-prefectural-museum-of-art|Minori Prefectural Museum of Art]] (1983) and [[harukawa-university|Harukawa University]] (1949) are both in Harukawa. The novelist [[michiyo-tono|Michiyo Tōno]] and the potter [[hanae-ishizuka|Hanae Ishizuka]] are the prefecture's best-known artists.

== Tourism ==
[[File:onsen-01.jpg|Inns along the stream at Tsukimi Onsen at dusk]]

Harukawa alone received 6.5 million tourist visitors in 2025. The main attractions are Harukawa Castle, whose keep had 512,000 visitors in FY2024; the Lantern Festival (380,000 in 2025); the Minori Bay Aquarium; and Tsukimi Onsen, with 23 inns and 612,000 overnight stays in 2024. The Tsukimi Ropeway (1968) climbs Mount Tsukimi, and the autumn colors of the mountain and the Kagami Gorge attract visitors from mid-October to mid-November.<ref name="kanko">Minori Prefecture Tourism Division (2025). ''Tourism Statistics of Minori Prefecture, 2024''. Minori Prefecture, pp. 5–15.</ref>
