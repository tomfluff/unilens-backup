'''Springvale Central Station''' (春川中央駅, ''Harukawa-Chūō-eki'') is the main railway station of [[harukawa|Springvale]], the capital of [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], Japan. It is the starting point of both lines of the [[harukawa-railway|Springvale Railway]] (Vale Rail): the [[kagami-line|Mirror Line]] to [[tsukimi-onsen|Moonview Spa]] and the [[bayside-line|Bayside Line]] to Ferry Port, and every [[tsukikage|Comet]] limited express starts and ends here. The station lies in the Ekimae district, a 15-minute walk south of [[harukawa-castle|Springvale Castle]], and with 38,420 boardings a day in FY2025 it is by far the busiest station on the network.<ref name="factbook">Springvale Railway (2026). ''Vale Rail Fact Book 2026''. Springvale Railway Co., p. 18.</ref>

The station opened on 15 July 1926 as '''Springvale Station''' (春川駅) and took its present name on 1 October 1962, when the Bayside Line opened. Its present elevated form and the station building '''Springvale Central Terrace''' (春川中央テラス), which contains shops and the railway's head office, opened on 26 March 2011.

== History ==
The station opened on 15 July 1926 as the terminus of the Mirror Valley Railway's first section, to Clayfield, with a small wooden building at the southern edge of the town.<ref name="hr100">Springvale Railway (2023). ''One Hundred Years of Vale Rail''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 30–33.</ref> The air raid of 29 July 1945 destroyed the building together with much of the station area, and a temporary wooden building served until a two-story concrete building was completed in 1953.{{cn}} The station square in front of it became the starting point of Sagimai Avenue, the 50 m wide boulevard of the postwar reconstruction plan, built from 1949 to 1958.

When the Bayside Line opened to Ferry Port on 1 October 1962, carrying both passengers and freight, a second island platform and a freight yard to the south were added for it, and the station was renamed Springvale Central. The freight yard closed with the end of freight traffic in 1987, and its site later became the plaza of the South Exit. In 1998 the Bayside Line was raised onto a viaduct between Springvale Central and Pier Road.

In 2004 Harvest Prefecture, the city of Springvale and Vale Rail began a joint project to raise the Mirror Line tracks onto a viaduct, which removed six level crossings at a cost of ¥24.8 billion.<ref name="pref2011">Harvest Prefecture Public Works Department (2011). ''Springvale Central Station Area Continuous Grade Separation Project: Completion Report''. Harvest Prefecture, pp. 3–11.</ref> The elevated station and Springvale Central Terrace opened on 26 March 2011, and the company moved its head office into the new building.<ref name="nippo2011">"New Springvale Central opens: 30,000 visit Terrace on first day". ''Springvale Herald'', 27 March 2011, p. 1.</ref>

== Layout ==
The station has five terminal tracks on the third level: two island platforms, for tracks 1 and 2 (Mirror Line) and tracks 5 and 6 (Bayside Line), and a side platform for track 3, used by the Comet. There is no track 4. The platforms are above a concourse with ticket gates on the second level that connects directly to the second floor of Springvale Central Terrace. Each platform is reached by stairs and elevators, and tactile paving and audible chimes guide passengers from the gates to the platforms.{{cn}}

== Exits and facilities ==
The station has three exits. The '''North Exit''' (北口) is the main entrance. Its plaza holds a bus terminal with 16 bays, used by Springvale City Bus and long-distance buses, and a taxi rank, and it faces the castle district and the Mirror River. Springvale Castle is about 15 minutes on foot, or 6 minutes by the City Bus 1 Castle Loop, which runs every 12 minutes; Castle Town, one stop by Mirror Line train, is the station nearest the castle. The '''South Exit''' (南口) leads to the concourse of the Bayside Line and has a taxi rank, the stop for highway coaches and the stop for the airport limousine bus to [[?Harvest Airport]], which runs every 30 minutes and takes 55 minutes (¥1,300). The '''East Exit''' (東口), open from 6:00 to 23:00, leads to the Station Square shopping arcade and to Springvale City Hall, eight minutes away.

Facilities in the station include:
* the Springvale Tourist Information Center, in the north concourse (8:30–19:00), with staff speaking English, Chinese and Korean;
* the RideCard service counter, in the second-floor concourse beside the central ticket gates, which issues and refunds [[haruca|RideCard]] cards and commuter passes;
* the ticket office (6:00–22:00), which sells Comet reservations from 5:30 to 23:00;
* 180 coin lockers.

'''Springvale Central Terrace''', the station building, has seven floors above ground and one below. About 85 shops and restaurants occupy the basement to the fourth floor; the basement food hall sells boxed tai-yuzu meshi and other [[cuisine-of-harukawa|local food]], and a gallery shop sells [[harukawa-ware|Springvale ware]].<ref name="terrace">Springvale Central Terrace (2025). ''Floor Guide''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 2–6.</ref> The Kumo Mobile Springvale Central shop is outside the building, one minute from the South Exit. The fifth floor has clinics and offices, and the sixth and seventh floors house the head office of Vale Rail.

== Surroundings ==
The North Exit opens onto Sagimai Avenue, the main street of the rebuilt city center, lined with hotels, banks and offices of the Ekimae district. The Mirror River promenade, one of the venues of the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Springvale Lantern Festival]], is about 5 minutes' walk from the North Exit; [[kagami-bridge|Mirror Bridge]], where the floating lanterns are released, and the Gofukumachi shopping arcade lie farther on. The East Exit leads to the Station Square shopping arcade and City Hall, and on the South Exit side are business hotels and the district of Bridge Street, served by the next station on the Bayside Line.
