'''Michiyo Tōno''' (遠野美千代, ''Tōno Michiyo''; 4 August 1914 – 19 November 1987) was a Japanese novelist and essayist from [[harukawa|Harukawa]], [[minori-prefecture|Minori Prefecture]]. A schoolteacher until 1946, she made her debut in 1949 and became nationally known with ''The River Mirror'' (1956), a novel about the Great Genbun Flood of 1739 and the first lantern memorial on the [[kagami-river|Kagami River]], which won the Kōyō Prize in 1957 and made the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Harukawa Lantern Festival]] famous across Japan. Her later works include the historical novel ''Heron Castle'' (1971) and the essay collection ''Tsukimi Diary'' (1980). She spent her last fifteen years at [[tsukimi-onsen|Tsukimi Onsen]], where the Michiyo Tōno Memorial Hall opened in 1992.<ref name="kaneda">Kaneda, Sachiko (2002). ''Michiyo Tōno: A Life by the River''. Minori Shuppan, pp. 3–9.</ref>

== Early life and teaching ==
Tōno was born on 4 August 1914 in Uomachi, the fishing quarter of Harukawa, where her father ran a small fish wholesale business. The date is taken from the family register; in her memoir ''Tsukimi Diary'' she gave her year of birth as 1915, and some reference works follow her.{{efn|The family register records 4 August 1914. In ''Tsukimi Diary'' (1980) Tōno wrote that she was born "in the summer of Taishō 4", that is 1915, and some encyclopedias and the 1960 film program give 1915. Her biographer Sachiko Kaneda suggests she confused her own birth with that of a younger brother who died in infancy.}}<ref name="kaneda"/>

She trained as a teacher at Minori Normal School, one of the predecessors of [[harukawa-university|Harukawa University]], graduating in 1933, and taught at elementary schools in Harukawa and in Kawamata village from 1933 to 1946. Her school in Shiomi was destroyed in the air raid of 29 July 1945, which killed 1,072 people in the city. She resigned from teaching in 1946 to write.<ref name="cityhist7">Harukawa City History Compilation Committee (1989). ''Harukawa City History'', vol. 7: ''Culture and Literature''. Harukawa City, pp. 388–401.</ref>

== Debut ==
Tōno's debut was the short story "Shiomi Bridge" (1949), published in the Harukawa literary magazine ''Minori Bungaku''. It follows a young teacher and a fisherman's son in the burned city after the air raid. A first collection of stories, ''The Salt Wind'', followed in 1953.

== ''The River Mirror'' ==
''The River Mirror'' (鏡の川, ''Kagami no kawa'', 1956) is set in Harukawa during the Great Genbun Flood of 4 October 1739, which killed about 1,140 people. Its heroine, Osayo, a fisherman's daughter from Uomachi, loses her family in the flood and, a year later, sets the first paper lantern on the river at the memorial ordered in 1740 by the sixth lord, [[asagiri-clan|Asagiri Tadatoki]]. The novel draws on the domain records of the flood and on the legend of the bronze mirror of [[?Kagami Shrine]].<ref name="kaneda"/>

The book won the Kōyō Prize in 1957 and became a best-seller. It was adapted as a film in 1960, with the lantern scenes filmed at the 1959 festival, and as an eight-part television drama in 1978. The two adaptations brought national attention to the Lantern Festival, and visitor numbers rose sharply in the 1960s.{{cn}}<ref name="nippo1960">"Film of The River Mirror premieres in Harukawa". ''Harukawa Nippō'', 2 October 1960, p. 3.</ref>

== Later works ==
''Lanterns at Low Tide'' (引き潮の灯, 1962) portrays oyster-farming families of [[minori-bay|Minori Bay]] at the time of the plans for the bay reclamation of 1961–1966, which they opposed. ''The Potter's Daughter'' (1966) is set among the kilns of Suyama in the 1930s; readers took it as a portrait of the potter [[hanae-ishizuka|Hanae Ishizuka]], but Tōno denied this.

From 1965 to 1972 Tōno lectured on modern Japanese literature at Harukawa University. In 1971 she published ''Heron Castle'' (鷺舞城, ''Sagimai-jō''), a historical novel about Asagiri Tadatoki and the fire that destroyed the keep of [[harukawa-castle|Harukawa Castle]] after a lightning strike on 15 June 1749. It is still sold at the castle's museum shop.<ref name="cityhist7"/>

== Tsukimi Onsen years ==
[[File:onsen-01.jpg|Tsukimi Onsen, where Tōno lived from 1972 until her death]]
In 1972 Tōno moved to the hot-spring town of Tsukimi Onsen, at the end of the [[kagami-line|Kagami Line]], and lived in an annex of the inn Seiryūkan (清流館) beside the river. There she wrote ''Valley of the Moon'' (1976), a novel set in the upper Kagami valley, and ''Tsukimi Diary'' (月見日記, ''Tsukimi nikki'', 1980), essays on her childhood in Uomachi, her teaching years and life in the valley below [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Tsukimi]]. She never married.<ref name="seiryukan">Seiryūkan (2012). ''A History of the Seiryūkan''. Seiryūkan, pp. 41–47.</ref>

== Death ==
Tōno died at Tsukimi Onsen on 19 November 1987, aged 73. Her grave is at [[?Kōun-ji]] in Harukawa.{{cn}} A last collection of stories, ''The Last Lantern'', was published in 1988.<ref name="nippo1987">"Novelist Michiyo Tōno dies at 73". ''Harukawa Nippō'', 20 November 1987, p. 1.</ref>

== Style and themes ==
Almost all of Tōno's fiction is set in Harukawa and the Kagami valley, and water runs through it: the river, the bay, floods, tides and the lanterns set adrift for the dead. Her heroines are mostly ordinary working women, fishermen's daughters, teachers and kiln workers, and her historical novels tell the story of the Asagiri domain from below rather than from the castle. Critics praised her plain, unhurried prose and her use of the Harukawa dialect in dialogue. Kaneda describes her as "the writer who taught Harukawa to remember its river".<ref name="kaneda"/>

== Legacy ==
=== Michiyo Tōno Memorial Hall ===
The Michiyo Tōno Memorial Hall (遠野美千代記念館) opened in Tsukimi Onsen in 1992, near the Seiryūkan. It displays her manuscripts, letters, desk and personal library, a reconstruction of her study at the inn, and material on the film and television adaptations of ''The River Mirror''.<ref name="hall1992">Tsukimi Town Board of Education (1992). ''Michiyo Tōno Memorial Hall: Guide''. Tsukimi Town, pp. 2–6.</ref>

=== Literary prize ===
The city of Harukawa has awarded the Michiyo Tōno Literary Prize (遠野美千代文学賞) every year since 1989 for unpublished short fiction. The award ceremony is held each October in the week of the Lantern Festival.<ref name="koho2019">Harukawa City (2019). "The 31st Michiyo Tōno Literary Prize". ''Kōhō Harukawa'', November 2019, p. 4.</ref>
