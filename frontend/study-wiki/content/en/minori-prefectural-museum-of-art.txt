The '''Harvest Prefectural Museum of Art''' (みのり県立美術館, ''Minori kenritsu bijutsukan'') is the prefectural art museum of [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], in the Sannomaru (third enclosure) of [[harukawa-castle|Springvale Castle]] in [[harukawa|Springvale]]. Opened in 1983, it holds about 9,600 works, including the largest public collection of [[harukawa-ware|Springvale ware]]. It received 186,000 visitors in fiscal year 2024.<ref name="pref2025">Harvest Prefecture Board of Education (2025). ''Annual Report of the Harvest Prefectural Museum of Art, FY2024''. Harvest Prefecture, pp. 3–8.</ref>

== History ==
Plans for a prefectural museum were made in the 1970s, when the prefecture acquired land in the former Sannomaru beside the castle's West Moat.{{efn|The Sannomaru was the outer enclosure of the castle, where senior retainers of the [[asagiri-clan|Asagiri clan]] had their residences. It is now occupied by the museum and civic buildings.}} The museum opened on 3 November 1983, Culture Day. It closed in April 2017 for renovation, which renewed the air conditioning, lighting and earthquake protection of the display cases, and reopened on 3 November 2018, its 35th anniversary.<ref name="nippo2018">"Museum of Art reopens after renovation". ''Springvale Herald'', 4 November 2018, p. 1.</ref>

== Architecture ==
The building was designed by the architect Shūji Kamiya (神谷修二). It is a long, low structure of exposed concrete with long glass walls that face a shallow reflecting pond, laid out so that the galleries do not compete with the castle walls beside them. The galleries are on two floors, with a glass corridor along the pond.<ref name="kamiya">Kamiya, Shūji (1984). "Harvest Prefectural Museum of Art". ''Architecture Japan Review'' 59 (2): 44–53.</ref>

== Collection ==
[[File:ware-02.jpg|Springvale ware storage jar with kawasuji brushwork, a type well represented in the collection]]
The collection of about 9,600 works covers Japanese painting, Western art, prints and the crafts of Harvest. Its 1,240 pieces of Springvale ware range from pieces of the Edo-period domain kiln and export ware to contemporary work. They include 312 works and the glaze notebooks of the potter [[hanae-ishizuka|Hanae Ishizuka]], donated by her family in 2010; a gallery devoted to her work opened in 2011.<ref name="mpma2011">Harvest Prefectural Museum of Art (2011). ''Hanae Ishizuka: The Donated Collection''. Exhibition catalog, pp. 2–3.</ref> Her jars "River Line" (1967) and "Moonview" (1985) are among the best-known works on display.

Outside the ceramics, the largest groups are woodblock prints and Japanese-style paintings of the [[kagami-river|Mirror River]], [[minori-bay|Harvest Bay]] and [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Moonview]] by artists from Harvest. The museum holds about four special exhibitions a year.
