The '''Mirror River''' (鏡川, ''Kagami-gawa'') is a Class A river in [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], Japan. It rises on the north slope of [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Moonview]] and flows 94 km{{efn|The length of 94 km is the figure of the national river authority, measured along the main stream from its source on Mount Moonview. The prefecture's own river register gives 96 km because it also counts the Hosoya headwater stream as part of the river.}} through the towns of Moonview and Mirror to [[minori-bay|Harvest Bay]] at [[harukawa|Springvale]], the prefectural capital. With a basin of 1,620 km², about 36% of the prefecture's area, it is the largest river in Harvest. Its clear, still pools gave it its name, "mirror river", and it is one of the cleanest rivers in western Japan.<ref name="office">Mirror River Office (2024). ''Outline of the Mirror River System''. National river administration, Springvale, pp. 2–5.</ref>

The river has shaped the history of Springvale, which was founded beside it as a castle town in 1603. The Great Genbun Flood of 4 October 1739 killed about 1,140 people there and gave rise to the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Springvale Lantern Festival]], when about 12,000 lanterns are floated on the river every October.

== Course ==
[[Map:kagami-river|The Mirror River, from Mount Moonview to Harvest Bay]]

The river rises at about 1,050 m on the north slope of Mount Moonview (1,214 m) in the town of Moonview. It circles the mountain to the east and then flows south through Moonview Valley, the upper valley in which the hot-spring town of [[tsukimi-onsen|Moonview Spa]] stands at 310 m. Below Firefly Valley it enters the town of Mirror and cuts through the Mirror Gorge (鏡峡), a narrow ravine below the old lava cliffs of the mountain, which ends near Red Canyon Station. At Futamata ("the fork") it is joined by its largest tributary, the Hosoya River (細谷川). Mean discharge there is 48.6 m³/s.<ref name="office"/>

Below Futamata the valley widens. The river passes the Ōkubo Dam and the yuzu orchards of Citrus Grove, enters Springvale at Ichinose and Kawamata, and flows past the pottery district of Suyama onto the Springvale plain. In the city it passes Sagimai Hill, on whose summit [[harukawa-castle|Springvale Castle]] stands on the left (east) bank 2.6 km from the mouth, and enters Harvest Bay through a small delta. The [[kagami-line|Mirror Line]] of the [[harukawa-railway|Springvale Railway]] follows the valley for almost all of its 38.6 km.

== Name and legend ==
The river's Japanese name, ''Kagami'' (鏡), means "mirror", which gives its English name, and the name is usually explained by the clear, still pools of the middle course, which reflect the sky and the surrounding hills. Before the castle town was built, the village at the mouth was called Kagami-no-tsu, "harbor of the Mirror", and the river also gave its name to the ancient Mirror District of Harvest Province.<ref name="okada">Okada, Shin'ichi (1978). "The place name Springvale and its origins". ''Journal of Harvest Local History'' 14: 21–34, p. 27.</ref>

A local legend tells of a princess who lost her bronze mirror in the river while crossing at Kagami-no-tsu. Years later a fisherman found the mirror in his net, shining as if new, and it was enshrined at [[?Mirror Shrine]] in Springvale, where it is still kept as the shrine's sacred object.<ref name="shishi5">Springvale City History Compilation Committee (1989). ''Springvale City History'', vol. 5: ''Folk Culture''. Springvale City, pp. 88–93.</ref>

== Floods and flood control ==
The lower river has always been prone to floods, especially during the rainy season and the typhoon season. In 1641–1648 the second lord of Springvale, [[asagiri-clan|Asagiri Masatsugu]], built the Masatsugu Embankment (正次堤), 3.2 km long, on the left bank to protect the castle town.

On 4 October 1739 (Genbun 4), after three days of typhoon rain, the river overtopped the embankment and flooded the lower town, killing about 1,140 people, most of them in Uomachi and Shiomi. The sixth lord, Asagiri Tadatoki, held the first lantern memorial in 1740 and had the embankment raised and strengthened.<ref name="morikawa">Morikawa, Junko (2014). ''Lanterns on the Water: Memory and Ritual on the Mirror River''. Springvale University Press, pp. 58–73.</ref> In September 1934 the Muroto Typhoon again flooded the low ground of Shiomi and Uomachi.{{cn}}

The Ōkubo Dam, a 46.5 m concrete gravity dam upstream of Citrus Grove Station in the town of Mirror, was completed in 1971 for flood control and to supply water to Springvale. On 19 September 2004 a typhoon brought 287.0 mm of rain to Springvale in one day, the highest daily total on record. The river overflowed in Shiomi, flooding about 2,300 homes, and washed away part of the Mirror Line embankment near Ichinose.<ref name="nippo2004">"Mirror River overflows in Shiomi; thousands of homes flooded". ''Springvale Herald'', 20 September 2004, p. 1.</ref> The national river office then raised and widened the embankments in Springvale under an emergency flood-control project completed in 2014.

== Bridges ==
[[File:train-01.jpg|A Comet limited express crossing the Futamata Bridge]]

The best-known crossing upstream is the Futamata Bridge (1929), a 218 m steel truss railway bridge designed by [[kenzo-hattori|Kenzō Hattori]], which carries the Mirror Line over the river just above the Hosoya confluence, below Red Canyon Station. In central Springvale three road bridges cross the river; from upstream to downstream they are:

During the Lantern Festival the floating lanterns are released just below Mirror Bridge and drift 1.4 km to Shiomi Bridge, where nets stretched across the river collect them.

== Ecology and fishing ==
The Mirror River is among the clearest rivers in western Japan: in 2023 its biochemical oxygen demand at Futamata was 0.5 mg/L.<ref name="env2023">Harvest Prefecture Environment Division (2024). ''Water Quality of Public Waters, FY2023''. Harvest Prefecture, p. 17.</ref> The river is known for ayu (sweetfish); the fishing season runs from 1 June to 31 October, and the Mirror River Fisheries Cooperative releases young ayu each spring. Red-spotted masu trout (''amago'') live in the upper reaches, and fireflies swarm along the stream near Firefly Valley in June. Grey herons and kingfishers, the birds of the prefecture and of Springvale, are common along the lower river. The upper valley and the Mirror Gorge lie in the Moonview–Mirror Prefectural Natural Park, designated in 1959.

== In culture ==
[[File:lanterns-01.jpg|Floating lanterns on the Mirror River at dusk during the Lantern Festival]]

The river is at the center of Springvale's main festival, the Springvale Lantern Festival, held on the second weekend of October. [[michiyo-tono|Michiyo Tōno]] made her debut with the short story "Shiomi Bridge" (1949), and her novel ''The River Mirror'' (1956) tells the story of the 1739 flood and the first lantern memorial. The wavy ''kawasuji'' ("river line") brushwork of [[harukawa-ware|Springvale ware]] is said to depict the river's currents, and feldspar from its bed is used in the ware's kasumi glaze. Grilled ayu from the river are the basis of ''kagami-jiru'', a clear soup named for its mirror-clear broth (see [[cuisine-of-harukawa]]).<ref name="ware">Suyama Kiln Association (2019). ''Springvale Ware: Four Hundred Years''. Suyama Kiln Association, p. 44.</ref>
