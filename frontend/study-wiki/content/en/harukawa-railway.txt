The '''Springvale Railway''' (春川鉄道, ''Harukawa Tetsudō''), commonly known as '''Vale Rail''' (ハルテツ), is a private railway company based in [[harukawa|Springvale]], the capital of [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], Japan. It operates two electrified lines with a combined length of 54.8 km and 22 stations: the [[kagami-line|Mirror Line]], which climbs 38.6 km up the valley of the [[kagami-river|Mirror River]] to the hot-spring town of [[tsukimi-onsen|Moonview Spa]], and the [[bayside-line|Bayside Line]], which runs 16.2 km along the north shore of [[minori-bay|Harvest Bay]] to Ferry Port. Both lines start at [[harukawa-central-station|Springvale Central Station]], whose station building also houses the company's head office. In fiscal year 2025 the railway carried about 41 million passengers, with 112,430 boardings a day; it had 684 employees in April 2026, and its operating revenue in FY2024 was ¥8.9 billion.<ref name="factbook">Springvale Railway (2026). ''Vale Rail Fact Book 2026''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 2–5.</ref>

The company was founded in 1923 as the '''Mirror Valley Railway''' (鏡谷鉄道, ''Kagamidani Tetsudō'') and opened its first section, from Springvale to Clayfield, in 1926 under its chief engineer [[kenzo-hattori|Kenzō Hattori]]. It took its present name in 1951. Its best-known train is the limited express [[tsukikage|Comet]], which links Springvale Central and Moonview Spa in 39 minutes. The company also issues the [[haruca|RideCard]] contactless IC card.

== History ==
{{main|history-of-harukawa}}

=== Mirror Valley Railway (1923–1951) ===
The Mirror Valley Railway was founded in 1923 with a capital of ¥300,000, subscribed mainly by merchants of Gofukumachi and Uomachi and by the Harvest Bank. Its promoters wanted to carry [[harukawa-ware|Springvale ware]] from the kilns of Suyama, and timber and charcoal from the upper valley, to the town and the port, and to bring visitors to the springs of Moonview Spa.<ref name="hr100">Springvale Railway (2023). ''One Hundred Years of Vale Rail''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 12–19.</ref> The board engaged Kenzō Hattori, a native of Uomachi who had spent 25 years with the government railways, as chief engineer. He planned a route along the river, built from the start to the 1,067 mm gauge of the national network. The first 13.5 km, from Springvale to Clayfield, opened on 15 July 1926, worked by four small steam tank locomotives imported from Germany.

The line then advanced up the valley in three stages: to Citrus Grove on 20 May 1928, across the new Futamata Bridge to Firefly Valley on 3 November 1929, and through the 2,184 m [[tsukimi-tunnel|Moonview Tunnel]] to Moonview Spa on 4 October 1931. The arrival of the railway turned Moonview Spa into a resort. Hattori was president of the company from 1931 to 1938. The air raid of 29 July 1945 destroyed the wooden Springvale Station building of 1926 and several cars, but trains were running again to Clayfield within a week.{{cn}}

=== Springvale Railway (1951–1986) ===
The company was renamed the Springvale Railway in 1951, and the Mirror Line was electrified at 1,500 V DC in 1952. On 1 October 1962 it opened the Bayside Line to Ferry Port, which carried both passengers and freight from the start and served the port and the [[?Springvale Shipyard]]; Springvale Station was renamed Springvale Central the same day. The line had been strongly promoted by the mayor [[tetsuo-saeki|Tetsuo Saeki]], whose city was spreading along the bay after the [[history-of-harukawa|1954 merger]] with four villages.<ref name="shishi">Springvale City History Compilation Committee (1989). ''Springvale City History'', vol. 4: ''Modern''. Springvale City, pp. 402–411.</ref> Double track on the Mirror Line was extended to Clayfield in 1968.

=== Comet and modernization (1987–2010) ===
In 1987 the company introduced the limited express Comet, using the new 6000 series, and freight traffic ended the same year. New rolling stock followed: the 2000 series for Mirror Line locals in 1996 and the 5000 series for the Bayside Line in 2004. In 1998 the Bayside Line was raised onto a viaduct between Springvale Central and Pier Road.

=== Since 2011 ===
On 26 March 2011 the company opened the elevated Springvale Central Station and its new station building, Springvale Central Terrace, and moved its head office there.<ref name="nippo2011">"New Springvale Central opens: 30,000 visit Terrace on first day". ''Springvale Herald'', 27 March 2011, p. 1.</ref> The RideCard card was introduced in 2012. The Comet 8000 series replaced the 6000 series on 16 March 2019. During the COVID-19 pandemic ridership fell to 29.8 million in FY2020, the lowest since the 1950s;{{cn}} by FY2025 it had recovered to 41.0 million, about 97 % of the FY2019 level. A phone version of the card, Mobile RideCard, followed in 2024.

A timetable revision on Saturday 14 November 2026 adds a stop at Trailhead to every Comet (41 minutes from end to end), stops rapid trains at Riverside, runs Bayside Line trains every 15 minutes instead of every 20 on weekdays between 10:00 and 16:40, and makes some last trains later on both lines. Fares, surcharges and pass prices do not change.<ref name="rev2026">Springvale Railway (2026). "Timetable revision on Saturday 14 November 2026". Notice, October 2026.</ref>

The two lines total 54.8 km and 22 stations, as Springvale Central is shared.{{efn|The Mirror Line has 14 stations and the Bayside Line 9; Springvale Central is counted once.}} Both are built to 1,067 mm gauge and electrified at 1,500 V DC. The Mirror Line is double track for its first 13.5 km to Clayfield and single track with passing loops beyond; its maximum speed is 95 km/h and its ruling gradient 25 ‰, between Firefly Valley and Moonview Spa. It is served by local trains (63 minutes from end to end), hourly rapid trains (55 minutes) and the Comet (39 minutes). The Bayside Line is double track and elevated for 5.0 km from Springvale Central to Pier Road; its trains stop at every station and take 28 minutes, running every 15 minutes in the weekday peaks and every 20 minutes on weekday middays and at weekends.

== Rolling stock ==
[[File:station-01.jpg|Springvale Central Station, the company's head office, at the North Exit]]
The company owns 46 cars of three series, all electric multiple units built by Nishiura Sharyō (西浦車輌) of Nishiura.<ref name="factbook"/> They are maintained at Clayfield Depot on the Mirror Line and at Seafront Depot on the Bayside Line.

The 8000 series was designed for 120 km/h but runs at no more than 95 km/h, the Mirror Line's limit. Retired series include the 1000 series of 1965, which the 2000 series replaced, and the 6000 series (1987–2019), the first Comet trains, whose set 6001 is preserved at Moonview Spa Station.

== Ridership ==
[[Chart:ridership|Annual passengers of the Springvale Railway, FY2016–FY2025 (millions)]]
The railway carried 43.9 million passengers in FY2016 and 42.4 million in FY2019 before the pandemic cut traffic to 29.8 million in FY2020. Numbers have risen every year since, to 33.0 million in FY2021, 37.3 million in FY2022, 39.6 million in FY2023, 40.4 million in FY2024 and 41.0 million in FY2025, still below the FY2019 level. In FY2025 there were 112,430 boardings a day: 38,420 at Springvale Central, 43,590 at the other Mirror Line stations and 30,420 at the other Bayside Line stations.<ref name="prefstats">Harvest Prefecture (2026). ''Harvest Prefecture Transport Statistics, FY2025''. Harvest Prefecture Planning Department, p. 37.</ref> The Comet carries about 400,000 passengers a year. Springvale Central is by far the busiest station; Firefly Valley, with 190 boardings a day, is the quietest. The busiest days of the year are the weekend of the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Springvale Lantern Festival]] in October.

== Fares and tickets ==
Fares are charged by distance, from ¥170 for up to 3 km to ¥970 for the longest journey, from Ferry Port to Moonview Spa. Fares paid with RideCard are up to ¥18 cheaper, and children pay half, rounded down to the nearest ¥10.{{efn|Distances are rounded up to the next whole kilometer, so the 3.6 km to University Gate is charged as 4 km.}} Fares were last revised on 1 October 2019, when the consumption tax rose to 10 %.<ref name="fares">Springvale Railway (2019). ''Passenger Fare Tariff'', revised edition. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 4–9.</ref>

All seats on the Comet are reserved and require a limited express surcharge on top of the fare: ¥520 for up to 25 km and ¥760 for 26 km or more, ¥200 more on peak dates and ¥260 more if bought on board. A seat in the Premium car costs ¥700 more. A trip from Springvale Central to Moonview Spa by Comet therefore costs ¥1,490, or ¥2,190 in the Premium car.<ref name="fares"/> The 1-Day Pass (¥1,300 on weekdays, ¥1,100 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays; children half) covers local and rapid trains on both lines but not the limited express surcharge, and the Moonview Spa Day Ticket (¥2,800) combines a return trip to Moonview Spa with a Comet seat each way, a visit to a public bath and a ¥500 voucher.

The [[haruca|RideCard]] IC card, introduced in 2012, can be used at every station and on Springvale City Bus and Moonview Bus, but not on the airport limousine bus. It requires a ¥500 deposit and holds up to ¥20,000; about 410,000 cards were in circulation in 2024.

== Connecting buses ==
Vale Rail does not operate buses. Local buses are run by the municipal Springvale City Bus, whose routes include the City Bus 1 Castle Loop from Springvale Central and City Bus 21 to the Clayfield Pottery Village, and in the upper valley by Moonview Bus, whose route 5 runs from Moonview Spa Station to the Moonview Ropeway on [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Moonview]] (25 minutes, ¥480, every 40 minutes, last bus 16:20). The airport limousine bus to [[?Harvest Airport]] leaves from the South Exit of Springvale Central every 30 minutes and takes 55 minutes (¥1,300).<ref>Springvale City Transportation Bureau (2026). ''Springvale City Bus Route Guide 2026''. Springvale City, p. 3.</ref>
