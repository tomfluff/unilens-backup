The '''Tsukikage''' (月影, "moonlight") is a limited express train service operated by the [[harukawa-railway|Harukawa Railway]] (HaruTetsu) on the [[kagami-line|Kagami Line]] in [[minori-prefecture|Minori Prefecture]], Japan. It links [[harukawa-central-station|Harukawa Central Station]] in [[harukawa|Harukawa]] with the hot-spring town of [[tsukimi-onsen|Tsukimi Onsen]], 38.6 km up the valley of the [[kagami-river|Kagami River]], in 39 minutes, against 55 minutes by rapid and 63 minutes by local train. It runs 7 trains to Tsukimi Onsen and 8 back on weekdays, and 9 and 10 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, and carries about 400,000 passengers a year.<ref name="factbook">Harukawa Railway (2026). ''HaruTetsu Fact Book 2026''. Harukawa Railway Co., p. 14.</ref>

The service began in 1987 with the 6000 series. Since 16 March 2019 it has been operated by three three-car 8000 series trains in navy blue with a gold stripe. All seats are reserved, and the Premium car offers wider seats and a forward view. Although the 8000 series was designed for 120 km/h, it never runs faster than 95 km/h, the maximum speed of the Kagami Line.

== Service pattern ==
The Tsukikage departs from track 3 at Harukawa Central. On weekdays northbound trains leave every two hours from 7:30 to 19:30, and southbound trains leave Tsukimi Onsen from 6:55 to 20:50; on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays trains run hourly to Tsukimi Onsen in the morning and back in the afternoon for day trippers to Tsukimi Onsen and [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Tsukimi]].<ref name="timetable">Harukawa Railway (2026). ''HaruTetsu Timetable, March 2026 edition''. Harukawa Railway Co., pp. 6–9.</ref> From Tsukimi Onsen Station, Tsukimi Bus route 5 runs to the Tsukimi Ropeway every 40 minutes (25 minutes, last bus 16:20). During the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Harukawa Lantern Festival]] the peak surcharge applies from 10 to 12 October 2026, and Tsukikage 21, 24, 25 and 28 do not stop at Shiromachi on 10 and 11 October.

Every train stops at Shiromachi, Kamano and Kagami-kyō. The line is single track beyond Kamano, and the Tsukikage passes local trains in the loops at Ōtsuka, Yuzuhara, Nakazawa and Tsukimi-guchi. From the timetable revision of 14 November 2026 all trains will also stop at Tsukimi-guchi, which lengthens the journey to 41 minutes; Tsukikage 1 on weekdays will leave Harukawa Central at 7:25 instead of 7:30 and arrive at 8:06 instead of 8:09. Surcharges do not change.<ref name="rev2026">Harukawa Railway (2026). "Timetable revision on Saturday 14 November 2026". Notice, October 2026.</ref>

== History ==
=== 6000 series (1987–2019) ===
HaruTetsu introduced the limited express Tsukikage in 1987, 35 years after the Kagami Line was electrified, mainly to carry holidaymakers to the inns of Tsukimi Onsen. The three five-car 6000 series sets built for it each included a panoramic lounge car called "Moonlight" that was open to all passengers. Trains at first took 52 minutes, and by the early 1990s there were six round trips a day.{{cn}} After track improvements in 1994 raised the line's limit to 95 km/h, the journey fell to 47 minutes.<ref name="hr100">Harukawa Railway (2023). ''One Hundred Years of HaruTetsu''. Harukawa Railway Co., pp. 141–158.</ref>

=== Since 2019 ===
The 8000 series entered service on 16 March 2019, the day after the last run of the 6000 series, and brought the present 39-minute timing. During the pandemic the service was cut to five round trips a day from April to June 2020.<ref name="nippo2020">"Tsukikage cut to five round trips". ''Harukawa Nippō'', 2 April 2020, p. 2.</ref> The added stop at Tsukimi-guchi from 14 November 2026 will make the journey 41 minutes.<ref name="rev2026"/>

=== 8000 series ===
The three 8000 series sets (8001–8003) were built by Nishiura Sharyō (西浦車輌) of Nishiura, which also built the company's other current trains. They have aluminum-alloy bodies painted navy blue with a gold stripe and seat 118 passengers: 24 in the Premium car (car 1), 38 in car 2, which also has two wheelchair spaces and a multipurpose toilet, and 56 in car 3, which has a luggage area.<ref name="nishiura">Nishiura Sharyō (2019). "HaruTetsu 8000 series limited express EMU". ''Nishiura Sharyō Technical Review'' 61: 4–19.</ref>

The trains were designed for 120 km/h because, when they were ordered, HaruTetsu planned to raise the speed limit between Kamano and Kagami-kyō to 110 km/h. That plan, estimated to cost ¥6.8 billion, was postponed indefinitely in 2017, and the 8000 series therefore runs at no more than the line's 95 km/h. The extra power is used instead for faster acceleration and on the 25 ‰ climb to Tsukimi Onsen, which is the main reason the journey is eight minutes shorter than with the 6000 series.<ref name="nippo2019">"New Tsukikage: built for 120, runs at 95". ''Harukawa Nippō'', 17 March 2019, p. 1.</ref>

== Premium car ==
The '''Premium''' car (プレミアム) is car 1, at the Tsukimi Onsen end of the train. Its 24 leather seats are arranged two plus one, with footrests and reading lights, and a panoramic window behind the driver's cab gives a forward view of the valley, the Futamata Bridge and the approach to the [[tsukimi-tunnel|Tsukimi Tunnel]]. It costs ¥700 on top of the limited express surcharge, also for children, and is often sold out on weekends in the autumn-color season and during the Lantern Festival.{{cn}}

== Fares ==
The limited express surcharge depends on the distance traveled on the Tsukikage: ¥520 for up to 25 km and ¥760 for 26 km or more (children ¥260 and ¥380), charged on top of the ordinary fare.{{efn|Kamano to Tsukimi Onsen is 25.1 km, which is rounded up to 26 km and therefore charged ¥760.}} On peak dates (10–12 October 2026, 28 December 2026 – 6 January 2027, 27 April – 6 May 2027 and 8–17 August 2027) it is ¥200 higher, and a surcharge bought on board costs ¥260 more. A trip from Harukawa Central to Tsukimi Onsen therefore costs ¥730 + ¥760 = ¥1,490, or ¥2,190 in the Premium car.<ref name="fares">Harukawa Railway (2019). ''Passenger Fare Tariff'', revised edition. Harukawa Railway Co., pp. 4–9.</ref> Reservations open at 10:00 one month before the travel date, at staffed stations, from reserved-seat ticket machines and online. A [[haruca|HaruCa]] card touched at the gates pays only the fare, but the surcharge can be paid from its balance at reserved-seat machines, and since 2024 Mobile HaruCa can also hold a ticketless Tsukikage seat reservation.

== Name ==
''Tsukikage'' is a literary word for "moonlight" or the light of the moon (literally "moon's shadow"). The name refers to [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Tsukimi]] (1,214 m),{{efn|Older maps and signs give 1,216 m; the height was revised after a national survey in 2014.}} the "moon-viewing mountain" over whose rounded summit the harvest moon rises when seen from [[harukawa-castle|Harukawa Castle]]. It was chosen in 1986 from 3,412 entries in a public naming contest.<ref name="hr100"/> The moon theme continued in the "Moonlight" lounge car of the 6000 series.
