The '''Mirror Line''' (鏡線, ''Kagami-sen'') is a 38.6 km railway line of the [[harukawa-railway|Springvale Railway]] (Vale Rail) in [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], Japan. It runs from [[harukawa-central-station|Springvale Central Station]] in [[harukawa|Springvale]] up the valley of the [[kagami-river|Mirror River]] to the hot-spring town of [[tsukimi-onsen|Moonview Spa]], serving 14 stations in the city of Springvale and the towns of Mirror and Moonview. It is double track for its first 13.5 km to Clayfield and single track beyond.

The line was planned by the engineer [[kenzo-hattori|Kenzō Hattori]] for the Mirror Valley Railway, as Vale Rail was called until 1951, and opened in four stages between 1926 and 1931; it was built to 1,067 mm gauge and electrified in 1952. Local trains take 63 minutes from end to end and rapid trains 55 minutes, and the limited express [[tsukikage|Comet]] covers the line in 39 minutes. In FY2025 its stations other than Springvale Central had 43,590 boardings a day, against 30,420 at those of the [[bayside-line|Bayside Line]].<ref name="factbook">Springvale Railway (2026). ''Vale Rail Fact Book 2026''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 6–9.</ref>

== Route ==
[[Chart:railmap|Route diagram of the Mirror Line and the Bayside Line]]
The line rises from about 4 m above sea level at Springvale Central to 310 m at Moonview Spa, following the Mirror River, which flows 94 km from [[mount-tsukimi|Mount Moonview]] to [[minori-bay|Harvest Bay]],{{efn|The length of 94 km is the figure of the national river authority; the prefecture's river register gives 96 km because it also counts the Hosoya headwater stream.}} for most of its length.

=== Springvale Central to Clayfield ===
Leaving the elevated platforms of Springvale Central, the line heads north-east through the city. Castle Town (1.8 km) is the nearest station to [[harukawa-castle|Springvale Castle]]: from its Castle Exit on the west side the keep is about eight minutes' walk uphill, and its River Exit on the east side leads to the Mirror River and a gallery street of [[harukawa-ware|Springvale ware]] shops. University Gate (3.6 km), opened in 1951, serves the Sagimai Campus of [[harukawa-university|Springvale University]], whose main gate is three minutes from the station, and the neighboring Medical Campus with the University Hospital, seven minutes on foot. Riverside (5.9 km) was added in 1968 for a new housing estate. Beyond Elmwood (8.2 km) the line follows the left (east) bank of the river past rice fields and orchards to West Meadow (10.7 km), between Kawamata and Ichinose, two former villages merged into Springvale in 1954.

Clayfield (13.5 km) is the station for the Suyama pottery district, where Springvale ware is made and where the [[harukawa-ware#Pottery Fair|Clayfield Pottery Fair]] is held every year on the first weekend of May in front of the station; the railway markets the district as the "Clayfield Pottery Village", which is also served by City Bus 21 from Springvale Central once an hour. The double track ends here, many local trains turn back, and the company's depot, Clayfield Depot, lies just beyond the station.

=== Clayfield to Red Canyon ===
Beyond Clayfield the single track enters the town of Mirror at the unstaffed Hilltop (16.4 km) and reaches Citrus Grove (19.8 km), surrounded by the terraced orchards that grow much of the prefecture's [[yuzu-cultivation-in-minori|yuzu]] and home to Springvale University's Citrus Grove Field Station. Upstream of Citrus Grove the line passes the Ōkubo Dam, and at Futamata, where the Hosoya River joins the Mirror just below Red Canyon Station, it crosses to the right bank on the Futamata Bridge.

=== Red Canyon to Moonview Spa ===
Red Canyon (23.6 km) stands at the mouth of the Mirror Gorge and is the start of the five-hour west trail up Mount Moonview. After the unstaffed Mill Creek (27.1 km), the last station in Mirror, the line reaches Firefly Valley (30.4 km), the first station in the town of Moonview, known for its fireflies in June; with 190 boardings a day it is the quietest on the line. From here the line climbs at up to 25 ‰ into the 2,184 m [[tsukimi-tunnel|Moonview Tunnel]], which takes it under a spur of Mount Moonview to Trailhead (34.9 km), where a statue of Hattori stands in front of the station.<ref name="tsukimi">Moonview Town History Committee (1994). ''Moonview Town History''. Moonview Town, pp. 286–291.</ref> The last 3.7 km run up the Moonview Valley to the terminus at Moonview Spa (38.6 km), from where Moonview Bus route 5 takes 25 minutes to the Moonview Ropeway.

== History ==
{{main|harukawa-railway}}
Hattori became chief engineer of the newly founded Mirror Valley Railway in 1923 and chose a route that kept close to the river, avoiding the hills on either side. The line was built to 1,067 mm gauge from the start and worked by steam. It opened in four stages:<ref name="hr100">Springvale Railway (2023). ''One Hundred Years of Vale Rail''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 21–64.</ref>

University Gate station opened on 1 April 1951, the year the company was renamed the Springvale Railway, and the line was electrified at 1,500 V DC in 1952. On 1 October 1968 the double track was completed as far as Clayfield and Riverside station opened. In 1994 track improvements raised the maximum speed from 85 km/h to the present 95 km/h.{{cn}} The flood of September 2004 washed away part of the embankment near Ichinose, and trains between Elmwood and Citrus Grove were replaced by buses for 23 days.<ref name="nippo2004">"Mirror Line to reopen on 13 October". ''Springvale Herald'', 9 October 2004, p. 2.</ref> When Springvale Central was rebuilt as an elevated station in 2011, the first 1.9 km of the line were raised onto a viaduct.

== Services ==
=== Local and rapid trains ===
Local trains, formed of two-car 2000 series sets, call at every station and take 63 minutes from end to end. On weekdays during the day they run every 30 minutes between Springvale Central and Clayfield, where every other train turns back, and hourly beyond Clayfield; in the morning and evening peaks through trains to Moonview Spa run every 30 minutes. Beyond Clayfield trains are driver-only, and passengers boarding at the unstaffed stations (Hilltop, Mill Creek and Firefly Valley) touch [[haruca|RideCard]] on the platform reader or take a boarding certificate from a machine on the platform.<ref name="factbook"/>

Rapid trains (快速, ''kaisoku'') run hourly between Springvale Central and Moonview Spa and take 55 minutes. They stop at every station except Riverside, West Meadow and Hilltop.<ref name="timetable">Springvale Railway (2026). ''Vale Rail Timetable, March 2026 edition''. Springvale Railway Co., pp. 2–5.</ref>

=== Comet ===
The limited express [[tsukikage|Comet]] runs 7 trains to Moonview Spa and 8 back on weekdays, and 9 and 10 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. It stops at Castle Town, Clayfield and Red Canyon and takes 39 minutes from Springvale Central to Moonview Spa. All seats are reserved; for the whole line the limited express surcharge is ¥760 on top of the ordinary fare of ¥730, or ¥1,490 in all. On the evenings of the [[harukawa-lantern-festival|Springvale Lantern Festival]] (10–11 October 2026) extra local trains run between Springvale Central and Clayfield or Red Canyon, and Comet 21, 24, 25 and 28 do not stop at Castle Town.

=== Timetable revision of November 2026 ===

Every Comet will also stop at Trailhead, which lengthens the journey to 41 minutes; Comet 1 on weekdays will leave Springvale Central at 7:25 and reach Moonview Spa at 8:06, instead of 7:30 and 8:09. Rapid trains will also call at Riverside, taking one minute longer. Last trains will run later: on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays the last train from Moonview Spa will leave at 21:40 (Springvale Central 22:43) instead of 21:05, and the last weekday northbound train from Springvale Central, at 23:20, will run to Red Canyon (arriving 23:59) instead of Clayfield. On the Bayside Line, weekday trains between 10:00 and 16:40 will run every 15 minutes instead of every 20. Fares, surcharges and pass prices do not change, and the new station timetables are published on 1 November.<ref name="rev2026">Springvale Railway (2026). == Infrastructure ==
[[File:train-01.jpg|A Comet 8000 series train on the Futamata Bridge]]
The '''Futamata Bridge''' (二股橋梁), completed in 1929 to Hattori's design, carries the line over the Mirror River just above its confluence with the Hosoya River, a short distance below Red Canyon Station. It is 218 m long, with three steel truss spans on masonry piers and short plate-girder approaches, and is the longest bridge on the Vale Rail network.<ref name="kuroda">Kuroda, Shin'ichi (2016). "Kenzō Hattori and the 25-per-mille rule". ''Railway History Review'' 42: 15–31, p. 22.</ref> It was strengthened for electrification in 1952 and was selected as civil engineering heritage by the Japan Society of Civil Engineers in 2008.{{cn}}

The '''Moonview Tunnel''' (2,184 m), between Firefly Valley and Trailhead, was bored from both ends between 1927 and 1931; the headings met on 12 December 1930. Fourteen workers died during its construction and are remembered by a memorial stone of 1932 at Trailhead. It was the longest tunnel in Harvest Prefecture until the Hiwada Road Tunnel opened in 1979.<ref name="hattori1932">Hattori, Kenzō (1932). "The construction of the Moonview Tunnel". ''Journal of the Japan Society of Civil Engineers'' 18 (4): 512–538.</ref>

The ruling gradient is 25 ‰ (1 in 40), on the climb between Firefly Valley and Moonview Spa. Hattori insisted on this limit, which he considered the most a steam locomotive could manage with a full train, even though it meant boring a long tunnel instead of a cheaper route over the ridge with gradients of 33 ‰.<ref name="kuroda"/> Single-track sections have passing loops at Hilltop, Citrus Grove, Red Canyon, Mill Creek and Trailhead.

== Rolling stock ==
* '''2000 series''' (since 1996): eight two-car sets (16 cars) in cream with a river-blue stripe, used on all local and rapid trains. They replaced the 1000 series of 1965.
* '''8000 series''' (since 2019): three three-car sets (9 cars) in navy blue with a gold stripe, used on the Comet. Built by Nishiura Sharyō for 120 km/h, they are limited to the line's 95 km/h.

Steam locomotives worked the line until electrification in 1952. The first Comet trains, the 6000 series, ran from 1987 to 2019; set 6001 is preserved at Moonview Spa Station.<ref name="hr100"/>
