'''Mount Moonview''' (月見山, ''Tsukimi-yama'', "moon-viewing mountain") is a dormant stratovolcano in the town of Moonview, [[minori-prefecture|Harvest Prefecture]], Japan, about 32 km north-east of central [[harukawa|Springvale]]. At 1,214 m it is the second-highest peak in the prefecture after Mount Hiwada (1,477 m), but its rounded cone, visible from most of the Springvale plain, makes it the best-known mountain in Harvest.{{efn|The height was revised from 1,216 m to 1,214 m after a national survey in 2014 measured the summit with satellite positioning. Older maps, guidebooks and some trail signs still give 1,216 m.}} The [[kagami-river|Mirror River]] rises on its north slope, and the hot-spring town of [[tsukimi-onsen|Moonview Spa]] lies at its southern foot.<ref name="gsi2014">Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (2014). ''Revised Elevations of Major Peaks, 2014''. p. 31.</ref>

== Geography and geology ==
[[Map:mount-tsukimi|Location of Mount Moonview in Harvest Prefecture]]
Mount Moonview is an andesite stratovolcano built by eruptions that began about 300,000 years ago. The summit, Meigetsu-dake (明月岳), is a lava dome from the last eruptive stage; the most recent eruption, about 24,000 years ago, produced lava flows on the south-west flank that now form the cliffs above the Mirror Gorge.<ref name="ishida">Ishida, Kazuo (2008). ''Volcanoes of Western Honshū''. Springvale University Press, pp. 144–159.</ref> Because it has not erupted in the last 10,000 years, it is not on the national list of active volcanoes; the heat of the old magma body is thought to feed the springs of Moonview Spa.

The [[kagami-river|Mirror River]] rises at about 1,050 m on the north slope and circles the mountain to the east before flowing south through Moonview Valley. The summit is about 7.5 °C colder than Springvale on average, and snow covers the upper slopes from December to March. Beech forest grows above about 900 m, with mixed maple and oak forest below.

== Name ==
The mountain is named for the moon. Seen from [[harukawa-castle|Springvale Castle]], the full moon of the 15th night of the 8th lunar month, the traditional night for moon viewing, rises over its rounded summit, and the Asagiri lords held moon-viewing parties in the castle keep to watch it. The summit's name, Meigetsu-dake, "bright-moon peak", has the same origin.<ref name="shishi5">Springvale City History Compilation Committee (1989). ''Springvale City History'', vol. 5: ''Folk Culture''. Springvale City, pp. 402–409.</ref>

== Religion ==
The summit has been a place of mountain worship since at least the Heian period. The upper shrine of Moonview Shrine stands on Meigetsu-dake, and its lower shrine is in Moonview Spa. The [[asagiri-clan|Asagiri]] lords of Springvale prayed there for rain in years of drought and rebuilt the upper shrine in 1702.{{cn}} The climbing season opens with a ceremony at the upper shrine on the first Sunday of May.

== Climbing ==
Pilgrims to the upper shrine used the Omote trail from the Edo period, and in the early 20th century the mountain became a favorite outing for students from Springvale. The arrival of the [[kagami-line|Mirror Line]] at Moonview Spa in 1931 brought day hikers from the city, and the first Meigetsu Hut was built in 1932; the present hut dates from 1987.{{cn}} On clear days the summit looks south over the Springvale plain to [[minori-bay|Harvest Bay]] and north to Mount Hiwada.

Three main trails reach the summit. The Meigetsu Hut, just below the summit, is staffed from May to November and has about 40 beds.<ref name="tsukimiguide">Moonview Town Tourism Association (2023). ''Moonview Spa Guide''. Moonview Town, pp. 30–35.</ref>

The west trail begins at Red Canyon Station on the [[kagami-line|Mirror Line]], and the Omote trail begins near Moonview Spa Station, the line's terminus.

=== Ropeway ===
The '''Moonview Ropeway''' (月見ロープウェイ), opened in 1968, climbs 2,140 m from Kōgen Station (580 m) to Sanjō Station (1,020 m) in 9 minutes. A round trip costs ¥2,200 for adults (one way ¥1,300; children half price). Cars leave every 20 minutes. Moonview Bus route 5 runs from Moonview Spa Station, the stop for the [[tsukikage|Comet]] limited express, to Kōgen Station in 25 minutes (¥480), every 40 minutes; the last bus leaves the station at 16:20.<ref name="ropeway">Moonview Ropeway Co. (2026). ''Fares and Timetable 2026''. Moonview Town, p. 2.</ref> The ropeway carries about 240,000 passengers a year, most of them in October and November.

== Seasons ==
Azaleas flower on the upper slopes in late May. In autumn the leaves turn first at the summit, from mid to late October, and the colors move down to Moonview Spa by early to mid November; the ropeway is busiest during these weeks. Winter ascents need crampons, and the ropeway runs year-round for views of the snow-covered summit.

== Natural park ==
The mountain is the center of the '''Moonview–Mirror Prefectural Natural Park''', designated in 1959. The park covers about 18,400 ha, including the summit area, Moonview Valley and the Mirror Gorge. Japanese serow, Asian black bears and copper pheasants live in its forests, and hikers are advised to carry bear bells.<ref name="park">Harvest Prefecture Environment Division (2019). ''Moonview–Mirror Prefectural Natural Park: 60th Anniversary Report''. Harvest Prefecture, pp. 4–12.</ref>
