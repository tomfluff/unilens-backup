    From today's featured article

    Springvale Castle is a hirayama-style Japanese castle on Sagimai Hill in Springvale, Harvest Prefecture. It was built between 1603 and 1611 by Asagiri Tadamasa, first lord of the Asagiri clan, and gave the city its castle town. The original five-tiered keep burned after a lightning strike on 15 June 1749 and was rebuilt by 1755, only to be demolished in 1874 when the castle buildings were sold off. The present keep, a reinforced-concrete reconstruction opened on 3 November 1966, was paid for in large part by citizens' donations collected under Mayor Tetsuo Saeki. Two original buildings survive: the Tatsumi Turret of 1609 and the Sagimai Gate of 1757. The castle grounds host the Harvest Prefectural Museum of Art and, every October, the processions of the Springvale Lantern Festival.

    On this day

    4 October: Events in the history of Springvale and Harvest Prefecture:

    • 1739 – The Great Genbun Flood of the Mirror River killed about 1,140 people in Springvale; the Lantern Festival began as their memorial the following year.
    • 1931 – The Mirror Valley Railway, today's Mirror Line, reached Moonview Spa with the opening of the 2,184-metre Moonview Tunnel.
    • 2015 – The Suyama Old Kiln, a 14-chamber climbing kiln of Springvale ware, was designated a National Historic Site.
