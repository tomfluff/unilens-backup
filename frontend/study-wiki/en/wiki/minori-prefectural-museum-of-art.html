    The Minori Prefectural Museum of Art (みのり県立美術館, Minori kenritsu bijutsukan) is the prefectural art museum of Minori Prefecture, in the Sannomaru (third enclosure) of Harukawa Castle in Harukawa. Opened in 1983, it holds about 9,600 works, including the largest public collection of Harukawa ware. It received 186,000 visitors in fiscal year 2024.[1]

    History[edit]

    Plans for a prefectural museum were made in the 1970s, when the prefecture acquired land in the former Sannomaru beside the castle's West Moat.[a] The museum opened on 3 November 1983, Culture Day. It closed in April 2017 for renovation, which renewed the air conditioning, lighting and earthquake protection of the display cases, and reopened on 3 November 2018, its 35th anniversary.[2]

    Architecture[edit]

    The building was designed by the architect Shūji Kamiya (神谷修二). It is a long, low structure of exposed concrete with long glass walls that face a shallow reflecting pond, laid out so that the galleries do not compete with the castle walls beside them. The galleries are on two floors, with a glass corridor along the pond.[3]

    Collection[edit]

    The collection of about 9,600 works covers Japanese painting, Western art, prints and the crafts of Minori. Its 1,240 pieces of Harukawa ware range from pieces of the Edo-period domain kiln and export ware to contemporary work. They include 312 works and the glaze notebooks of the potter Hanae Ishizuka, donated by her family in 2010; a gallery devoted to her work opened in 2011.[4] Her jars "River Line" (1967) and "Tsukimi" (1985) are among the best-known works on display.

    Outside the ceramics, the largest groups are woodblock prints and Japanese-style paintings of the Kagami River, Minori Bay and Mount Tsukimi by artists from Minori. The museum holds about four special exhibitions a year.
