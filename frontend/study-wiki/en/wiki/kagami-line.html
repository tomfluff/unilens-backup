    The Mirror Line (鏡線, Kagami-sen) is a 38.6 km railway line of the Springvale Railway (Vale Rail) in Harvest Prefecture, Japan. It runs from Springvale Central Station in Springvale up the valley of the Mirror River to the hot-spring town of Moonview Spa, serving 14 stations in the city of Springvale and the towns of Mirror and Moonview. It is double track for its first 13.5 km to Clayfield and single track beyond.

    The line was planned by the engineer Kenzō Hattori for the Mirror Valley Railway, as Vale Rail was called until 1951, and opened in four stages between 1926 and 1931; it was built to 1,067 mm gauge and electrified in 1952. Local trains take 63 minutes from end to end and rapid trains 55 minutes, and the limited express Comet covers the line in 39 minutes. In FY2025 its stations other than Springvale Central had 43,590 boardings a day, against 30,420 at those of the Bayside Line.[1]

    Route[edit]

    The line rises from about 4 m above sea level at Springvale Central to 310 m at Moonview Spa, following the Mirror River, which flows 94 km from Mount Moonview to Harvest Bay,[a] for most of its length.

    Springvale Central to Clayfield[edit]

    Leaving the elevated platforms of Springvale Central, the line heads north-east through the city. Castle Town (1.8 km) is the nearest station to Springvale Castle: from its Castle Exit on the west side the keep is about eight minutes' walk uphill, and its River Exit on the east side leads to the Mirror River and a gallery street of Springvale ware shops. University Gate (3.6 km), opened in 1951, serves the Sagimai Campus of Springvale University, whose main gate is three minutes from the station, and the neighboring Medical Campus with the University Hospital, seven minutes on foot. Riverside (5.9 km) was added in 1968 for a new housing estate. Beyond Elmwood (8.2 km) the line follows the left (east) bank of the river past rice fields and orchards to West Meadow (10.7 km), between Kawamata and Ichinose, two former villages merged into Springvale in 1954.

    Clayfield (13.5 km) is the station for the Suyama pottery district, where Springvale ware is made and where the Clayfield Pottery Fair is held every year on the first weekend of May in front of the station; the railway markets the district as the "Clayfield Pottery Village", which is also served by City Bus 21 from Springvale Central once an hour. The double track ends here, many local trains turn back, and the company's depot, Clayfield Depot, lies just beyond the station.

    Clayfield to Red Canyon[edit]

    Beyond Clayfield the single track enters the town of Mirror at the unstaffed Hilltop (16.4 km) and reaches Citrus Grove (19.8 km), surrounded by the terraced orchards that grow much of the prefecture's yuzu and home to Springvale University's Citrus Grove Field Station. Upstream of Citrus Grove the line passes the Ōkubo Dam, and at Futamata, where the Hosoya River joins the Mirror just below Red Canyon Station, it crosses to the right bank on the Futamata Bridge.

    Red Canyon to Moonview Spa[edit]

    Red Canyon (23.6 km) stands at the mouth of the Mirror Gorge and is the start of the five-hour west trail up Mount Moonview. After the unstaffed Mill Creek (27.1 km), the last station in Mirror, the line reaches Firefly Valley (30.4 km), the first station in the town of Moonview, known for its fireflies in June; with 190 boardings a day it is the quietest on the line. From here the line climbs at up to 25 ‰ into the 2,184 m Moonview Tunnel, which takes it under a spur of Mount Moonview to Trailhead (34.9 km), where a statue of Hattori stands in front of the station.[2] The last 3.7 km run up the Moonview Valley to the terminus at Moonview Spa (38.6 km), from where Moonview Bus route 5 takes 25 minutes to the Moonview Ropeway.

    History[edit]

    Main article: Springvale Railway

    Hattori became chief engineer of the newly founded Mirror Valley Railway in 1923 and chose a route that kept close to the river, avoiding the hills on either side. The line was built to 1,067 mm gauge from the start and worked by steam. It opened in four stages:[3]

    Opening of the Mirror Line
    DateSectionLengthNotes
    15 July 1926Springvale – Clayfield13.5 kmSteam; Castle Town, Elmwood and West Meadow opened at the same time
    20 May 1928Clayfield – Citrus Grove6.3 kmHilltop
    3 November 1929Citrus Grove – Firefly Valley10.6 kmFutamata Bridge; Red Canyon and Mill Creek stations
    4 October 1931Firefly Valley – Moonview Spa8.2 kmMoonview Tunnel; Trailhead station[b]

    University Gate station opened on 1 April 1951, the year the company was renamed the Springvale Railway, and the line was electrified at 1,500 V DC in 1952. On 1 October 1968 the double track was completed as far as Clayfield and Riverside station opened. In 1994 track improvements raised the maximum speed from 85 km/h to the present 95 km/h.[citation needed] The flood of September 2004 washed away part of the embankment near Ichinose, and trains between Elmwood and Citrus Grove were replaced by buses for 23 days.[4] When Springvale Central was rebuilt as an elevated station in 2011, the first 1.9 km of the line were raised onto a viaduct.

    Services[edit]

    Local and rapid trains[edit]

    Local trains, formed of two-car 2000 series sets, call at every station and take 63 minutes from end to end. On weekdays during the day they run every 30 minutes between Springvale Central and Clayfield, where every other train turns back, and hourly beyond Clayfield; in the morning and evening peaks through trains to Moonview Spa run every 30 minutes. Beyond Clayfield trains are driver-only, and passengers boarding at the unstaffed stations (Hilltop, Mill Creek and Firefly Valley) touch RideCard on the platform reader or take a boarding certificate from a machine on the platform.[1]

    Rapid trains (快速, kaisoku) run hourly between Springvale Central and Moonview Spa and take 55 minutes. They stop at every station except Riverside, West Meadow and Hilltop.[6]

    Comet[edit]

    The limited express Comet runs 7 trains to Moonview Spa and 8 back on weekdays, and 9 and 10 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. It stops at Castle Town, Clayfield and Red Canyon and takes 39 minutes from Springvale Central to Moonview Spa. All seats are reserved; for the whole line the limited express surcharge is ¥760 on top of the ordinary fare of ¥730, or ¥1,490 in all. On the evenings of the Springvale Lantern Festival (10–11 October 2026) extra local trains run between Springvale Central and Clayfield or Red Canyon, and Comet 21, 24, 25 and 28 do not stop at Castle Town.

    Timetable revision of November 2026[edit]

    On Saturday 14 November 2026 Vale Rail will revise the timetable. Every Comet will also stop at Trailhead, which lengthens the journey to 41 minutes; Comet 1 on weekdays will leave Springvale Central at 7:25 and reach Moonview Spa at 8:06, instead of 7:30 and 8:09. Rapid trains will also call at Riverside, taking one minute longer. Last trains will run later: on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays the last train from Moonview Spa will leave at 21:40 (Springvale Central 22:43) instead of 21:05, and the last weekday northbound train from Springvale Central, at 23:20, will run to Red Canyon (arriving 23:59) instead of Clayfield. On the Bayside Line, weekday trains between 10:00 and 16:40 will run every 15 minutes instead of every 20. Fares, surcharges and pass prices do not change, and the new station timetables are published on 1 November.[7]

    Infrastructure[edit]

    The Futamata Bridge (二股橋梁), completed in 1929 to Hattori's design, carries the line over the Mirror River just above its confluence with the Hosoya River, a short distance below Red Canyon Station. It is 218 m long, with three steel truss spans on masonry piers and short plate-girder approaches, and is the longest bridge on the Vale Rail network.[8] It was strengthened for electrification in 1952 and was selected as civil engineering heritage by the Japan Society of Civil Engineers in 2008.[citation needed]

    The Moonview Tunnel (2,184 m), between Firefly Valley and Trailhead, was bored from both ends between 1927 and 1931; the headings met on 12 December 1930. Fourteen workers died during its construction and are remembered by a memorial stone of 1932 at Trailhead. It was the longest tunnel in Harvest Prefecture until the Hiwada Road Tunnel opened in 1979.[9]

    The ruling gradient is 25 ‰ (1 in 40), on the climb between Firefly Valley and Moonview Spa. Hattori insisted on this limit, which he considered the most a steam locomotive could manage with a full train, even though it meant boring a long tunnel instead of a cheaper route over the ridge with gradients of 33 ‰.[8] Single-track sections have passing loops at Hilltop, Citrus Grove, Red Canyon, Mill Creek and Trailhead.

    Rolling stock[edit]

    • 2000 series (since 1996): eight two-car sets (16 cars) in cream with a river-blue stripe, used on all local and rapid trains. They replaced the 1000 series of 1965.
    • 8000 series (since 2019): three three-car sets (9 cars) in navy blue with a gold stripe, used on the Comet. Built by Nishiura Sharyō for 120 km/h, they are limited to the line's 95 km/h.

    Steam locomotives worked the line until electrification in 1952. The first Comet trains, the 6000 series, ran from 1987 to 2019; set 6001 is preserved at Moonview Spa Station.[3]
