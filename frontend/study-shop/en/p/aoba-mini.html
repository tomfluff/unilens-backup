Product description

The Aoba Mini is a small, light rice cooker for one or two people, a student room, or a second cooker for the office. It cooks up to three Japanese rice cups (gō), about six bowls of rice, and takes up about as much space as a large saucepan.

It is a microcomputer cooker, not IH: a 350 W heater plate under the pot does the work and a sensor adjusts the heat. It uses less than a third of the power of an IH cooker, and the rice is good, but a little softer and less even. At the full 3 cups the bottom layer can turn firm and starchy water can bubble out of the vent; for two people who eat rice every day, cooking 2 cups at a time gives the best results.

There is no brown-rice menu, and brown rice cooked on the white setting stays hard. Porridge is limited to 0.5 cup of rice, about two bowls. The steam tray sits above the rice, so you can steam vegetables or reheat frozen gyoza while 1–2 cups of rice cook; with 3 cups the tray touches the rice.

Wash the inner pot and inner lid after each use with a soft sponge. The water lines inside the pot are printed in silver on the grey coating and can be hard to see; if so, measure 200 mL of water per cup of rice with a jug. Beige and Mint are identical apart from colour. Aoba covers the cooker for one year.

Is this an IH rice cooker?
Answer:
No. The Aoba Mini is a microcomputer rice cooker: a 350 W heater plate under the pot heats from the bottom and a microcomputer controls the heat. IH cookers heat the whole pot by induction, which cooks more evenly but needs over 1,000 W.
