Product description

The Kaze Open-Ear sit just in front of your ears, on the cheekbones, and carry sound to the inner ear as vibration. Your ear canals stay open, so you hear cars, bicycles and announcements as usual. That makes them popular with runners and cyclists, with people who find earbuds painful, and with anyone who needs to hear a doorbell or a child while listening.

The trade-offs: bass is lighter than with earbuds or over-ear headphones, and at the top few volume steps you may feel a tickling vibration. In quiet rooms, people sitting next to you can hear the sound at high volume, and on a noisy train you will struggle to hear podcasts because nothing blocks outside noise. Foam earplugs are included for when you want to shut out the world and listen only through the band.

Charging uses a magnetic cable that clicks onto two contacts on the right ear hook, so there is no open port to let water in. The other end is USB-A: plug it into a USB-A charger, a computer, or a power bank such as the Ampora Slim 10K. To charge from a laptop with only USB-C ports, use a USB-C to USB-A adapter. Take the cable with you when you travel, because no other cable fits; spare cables cost ¥1,280 from Kaze support.

Multipoint connects two devices at once, for example your phone and a work laptop. IP67 means the headphones survive 30 minutes under 1 m of fresh water, so rain, sweat and rinsing are fine, but they are not made for swimming. Whether you may cycle with headphones depends on local rules, so check before you ride. Kaze covers the headphones and cable for one year from purchase.
